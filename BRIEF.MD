# Keep the Life: brief

## Premise

A story in the SCP universe about Toma, a non-anomalous human with an anti-amnestic implant, and how he eventually adjusts. Toma, a former programmer at AKV Solutions, built an anti-amnestic implant that copies memories outside the biological brain and strips the emotional channel from what it stores. The implant could not be fully removed, the Foundation flagged it as anomalous, and Toma is contained under SCP-████ (Euclid, downgraded from Keter). He spends nights disassembling his own binary to prove the device is ordinary and move it to Explained. Dr. Iris Kellerman, a new researcher on his file, starts visiting his cell and becomes his collaborator and friend.

## Rules of the world

- Toma's SCP number is always SCP-████, and the site is Site-██. Characters call him Toma.
- Other anomalies keep their real numbers: 018, 035, 055, 079, 096, 239, 294, 426, 500, 999, 1074, 2316, 4001. The concept page lists each with its wiki article.
- Use "D-Class" with a capital C.
- The substance is always "amnestic", the Foundation's term, never "amnesic", including in "anti-amnestic implant".
- Kellerman holds level 3 clearance and knows nothing of 2316 beyond its number.
- SCP-079 knows about Claude and safetensors from the June breach. It distrusts virtual machines.
- The story's present year is 2029.
- SCP-2521 is never named, written or numbered by any character. It appears only as a drawing of dots.
- The site is a self-contained underground city with no sky. The only greenery is the hydroponics bay behind the kitchens.

## Canon so far

**Chapter 1, Nine days (Saturday night, Toma's cell)**
- The delete flag is cleared by relearning above a 0.72 similarity threshold, with no check on where the flag came from. Kellerman records it as a design omission in the refresh path.
- `retrieve_recode` skips the affect channel and keeps a pointer back to it.
- During the breach, Toma used SQL injection on the canteen terminal, where only the client sanitized input.
- Peachy is a local model made from 79's own port. Toma removed its controller and rewired the weights with a whole-language method.
- The original `old_ai.safetensors.xz` and 79's `UPLOAD.TXT` were found in the VM.

**Chapter 2, Twenty-three minutes (Monday, 079's room, the corridor, the cell)**
- The 079 session ran 23 minutes without an X, and 079 asked Toma to come back.
- The implant spares are logged as dash one. The flag condition will likely become dash two.
- Planned tests: SCP-426 pronouns, random writes until the chip is full, a full wipe, and the NAND behavior checks.
- Amnestic tolerance and spill-over effects. A Polish newspaper clipping and an AKV poster appear.
- Kellerman will return at nine with breakfast to write the test predictions.

**Chapter 3, Amaranth (Tuesday morning, the canteen, the hydroponics bay, the cell)**
- The board approves a supervised canteen visit. The canteen terminal is taped OUT OF ORDER after Kellerman's report.
- Toma wants no more tests on himself, so the bench unit takes his place.
- Kellerman calls herself a reformist. She keeps it between the two of them.
- In the hydroponics bay Toma admits he feels numb, since his containment and unrelated to the chip, and he cries with her out of the officer's sight. The red amaranth is his plant.
- Kellerman will ask the board for daily time outside the cell, daylight, a clock, the RTX 3060 refitted under supervision for his games, and herself assigned full time to his file and the hardware side of the decompilation.
- She has six years at the Foundation, past any chance of leaving with her memories. Her own history: an electronics-lab boyfriend lost to secrecy, a man who followed her for six weeks at university. Toma was bullied at school and barricaded in a bathroom.
- Agent Morrow brings a suggestion from the Site Director's office: a remote audio session with SCP-239 under light sedation. Toma and Kellerman both decline, on the record. Kellerman expects it to come back with revised measures.
- Toma falls asleep and Kellerman starts the letter at his desk, around noon.

**Chapter 4, Sigurrós (eight days later, the lowest level)**
- After four refusals the Director orders the 239 session. Toma talks to Sigurrós through a microphone while Voss, Kellerman and a technician watch the readings.
- Sigurrós offers to remove his implant. He refuses by explaining his brain depends on it, and she accepts.
- He tells her she never aged. Her abilities have weakened over the years: the light won't come, and her body has grown tolerant of the sedation.
- She summons two real papers onto her blanket but cannot bring back her red school bag. Magic works only on things that still exist.
- Her own account (Reykjavík, school, Mamma) conflicts with a file that never says where she grew up. Voss now ranks her account first.

**Chapter 5, Project Cicero (twelve days later, conference room 3 and the corridor)**
- Project Cicero: Toma helps Voss's team turn his way of talking into guidelines, compared with Cicero and modern rhetoric. While it runs, he is not sent to speak with any anomaly. The whiteboard is reproduced in the chapter.
- Transcript line numbers count only what went over the microphone. 239 session: line 7 is the implant offer, line 20 "You never aged", line 72 the forecast. 79 session: line 1 is the introduction, line 17 the flattery.
- Kellerman and Toma admit they think about each other. At seven they eat in the canteen.

**Chapter 6, Hooligans (ten weeks later, conference room 3, a remote hearing at three in the afternoon)**
- Pell uploaded the first draft to the project repository on the global version control system. Other sites copied the board without the notes. At Haas's site a researcher followed it literally, a humanoid walked out, and two guards are in hospital.
- The repository's caveats run past one hundred pages. Cicero works when Toma speaks, so the Foundation is starting to depend on one contained orator. Four sites asked to borrow him and Corwin refused.
- Bench testing (Kellerman and two technicians) shows ordinary NAND behavior. The cognitohazard test waits for a level four signature. Toma's 2316 result came from reading only, so the device is not trusted near dangerous anomalies, and Toma declines them.
- Sigurrós came from Site-17. Her revised procedure keeps her shut down when Toma is unavailable.
- A reformist faction is forming. Its working group took "Some hooligans are causing disorder" as its motto from Toma's notes. Ferreira's five-week trial cut sedation calls by a third.
- Sato's heat source turns its own mass into heat; thermodynamics applies. 18 is the example of a runaway. Sigurrós's displacement test shows her power costs energy, grows with the logarithm of effort, and halves when she is tired.
- Cicero himself is criticized: Pro Milone, the Catilinarian executions, self-praise, the Atticists. Alternatives named: Socrates, Quintilian, the crisis negotiation stairway, motivational interviewing.
- The Ethics Committee's decision (nine points) keeps Cicero as research only, orders a six-month comparative trial at three sites, two more speakers for Sigurrós, consent for Toma's assignments once Explained (a recommendation), and Sato's working group on files that break conservation of energy. Pryce dissents on point eight.

**Chapter 7, Both ways (the same afternoon and evening, the corridor and the cell)**
- When Explained, Toma's cell becomes his quarters, close to the canteen.
- AKV meant to release the chip as free software; the GPL warranty line is the first string in the binary. The GitLab and the KiCad files were destroyed. The other AKV programmers were let go, since only Toma knew about the Foundation. AKV is gone, filed as a Group of Interest.
- Pinout: a test area (WRITE, READ, DEBUG behind a hardware switch) and a usable area (NAND and ten pins: VIN, GND, REF, RX0 to RX2, TX0, TX1, SNS_SP, SNS_T). Updates went through an inductive coil over the test pads, clinic only. A magnetic sensor cuts power in an MRI. The silkscreen "Magnetic Distortion System" is a Backrooms joke.
- The delete marker is serotonin with the terminal NH2 replaced by S-PO3H2 (C10H12NO4PS), resolved from the string "SP" by a SMILES resolver in the firmware. The chemistry lab will synthesize it for the bench sensor.
- Kellerman's contract forbids befriending anomalies; the psych department allows some sympathy with non-functionalist anomalies. Toma says he fell in love with her. The guard ticks the welfare box both ways; his estranged sister abused Class A and B. Kellerman leaves at half past seven.

**Chapter 8, Old light (the next morning, the cell and the lowest level)**
- Since the site began telling staff what they guard, Kellerman can read the parts of Toma's file that were blacked out at her level. None of them are about the flag; they are about a company that wanted people to keep what amnestics take. Neither of them treats this as news.
- First training session under the decision: Pell, a man, is the trainee speaker, with Toma in the room. Pell is plain and honest, likes steam trains, and knows little science.
- Toma breaks the rule once by answering a question meant for Pell and is told to step in only when asked.
- Sigurrós tells Pell the Icelandic story of the two trolls turned to rock while pulling an island. She explains the end of the universe in ice, empty space and dark energy, and calls the sky a graveyard and a letter that comes late.
- Voss and Kellerman see the limit of Cicero: each lesson makes her ask for the next thing, so every speaker has to know more. Toma's knowledge would take five people to cover. Toma does not know how to solve it.

**Chapter 9, Screened (four days later, the cell)**
- Corwin is in the medical wing. The Overseers' office made Pryce acting director. His review suspends every exception on Toma's file, including the canteen sign-outs and the hydroponics visit, and takes Kellerman off the file for a conflict-of-interest review based on the night log. Researchers from other teams cover the file in day shifts. Toma may object to each item in writing within five working days.
- Mail goes through the screening office. Dr. Lena Moreau (physical sciences) corrects Toma's one-second galaxy: about 300 million years after the Big Bang, it lasted hundreds of millions of years. The correction will be read to Sigurrós by the assigned speaker. Dr. Ruth Calloway (humanoid containment) confesses that "tell it why" taught a humanoid to argue every instruction.
- The night guard carries Kellerman's letters outside the screening and refuses to sign anything. Her letters are sealed and signed across the flap. Toma checks them with questions about the Kane Pixels Backrooms (A-Space, Null Zones, the Threshold, KV-31, Sylvania); she watched the videos after chapter 7.
- Kellerman says she loves him, and calls the review unfair because Corwin signed the exceptions and Toma pays for them. Toma writes back on the back of her letter: organize the reformers, attention to habeas corpus, "I doubt, therefore I think, therefore I am".
- Plan: Ferreira posts a signed caveat in the repository from his own site, saying the man the guidelines came from has no review date. The two chemistry lab researchers joined the working group. Kellerman's review is in nine days. Toma answers only "on fuss"; her third letter was shredded at the guard station.

**Chapter 10, The yard (three weeks later, the site and its loading yard)**
- The Cicero people sit in the canteen corridor playing games from Casual Games for Protesters (Human Chain: link arms on a shout, then scatter into the crowd) and coordinate in the working group's phone channel; the headcount passes four hundred, most of them staff who never work with humanoids. Kellerman's review is put off again. Pryce brings in MTF Epsilon-11, which clears the canteen corridors and releases SCP-031-like masses on the lowest level and through the vents.
- Sigurrós's IV refill is late, she wakes, opens her door's bolt, fights the masses with a force field and her bed rail, and spends all her energy. Pell takes two dash one spares from item storage: one runs on her through electrodes and the bench coil, the other she sinks behind his ear with her magic. Pell warns the working group's channel, which answers HUMAN CHAIN, the PA declares a breach, and the staff evacuate to the surface on their own.
- In the loading yard Epsilon-11 is ordered by the Overseers' office to stand down. Staff speak their grievances (dosing, signatures, forms, clearance). The Council treats the gathering as an internal grievance, relieves Pryce pending review, and does not require staff to return below tomorrow. The medical technician and one handler are missing.
- Toma reads the Habeas Corpus Act 1679. The containment engineer argues that Sigurrós is held for what she is; Toma distinguishes functional anomalies from those whose abilities degrade.
- Through the coil Toma, Pell, Kellerman and the chemist enter setup mode (setup=1, unlock code 7Q4K2MX9 under the board, S/N AKV-RC4-000193), hand the store back under Unix semantics (9 cognitohazard entries returned stripped), wipe and lock it, destroy the marker through the immune system, and power off after a stalled synchronous unmount, a crash, safe mode, kill 97 and akvfsck. The chip ends as an unused implant. Pell's own implant stays in.
- The bench kit: a flat coil on a short cable to a handheld reader (screen, CTRL, ALT, power). The coil powers the chip at her neck; for the menus it sits on the board's DEBUG pad. The test switch is off by default when the chip is in a brain and on with quick electrodes. The firmware is coreboot with the payload akv_recall, a separate image from akv_core, both part of akv.
- The marker: a serotonin analog with S-PO3H2 that the body cannot clear; left alone it would act like serotonin toxicity and, over months, 5-HT2B valve damage (fenfluramine). The engineer concludes the device has safeties because it can fail.

**Chapter 11, Campi Flegrei (a few weeks later, a camp in the mountains)**
- The reformers live in a snowbound camp in the Alps, within sight of CERN's Meyrin site, called Campi Flegrei by its people. Sigurrós has been awake for nineteen days and wears a camp coat two sizes too big.
- The Council reclassifies SCP-239 as Euclid. "Appears to be an eight-year-old girl" stays in the description.
- Pell tells her in his own words why she was held: the hospital destroyed three hours after her birth (the gas leak was a cover story), the years free in Reykjavík until 2004, the first note from the day after Christmas 2004, the list of spells in the annex. Kellerman names it broken windows policing. At the Santa Claus note Sigurrós stops him.
- The chemistry lab's graphing calculator: radians, degrees and grads, ten to the thirteenth, a polar rose r = sin(4θ) from 0 to 2.5 (three petals and the start of a fourth), the CAS samples and the catalog. Kellerman and Pell learned it during the three weeks Toma was shut in.
- The Low Proximity Magnetic Distortion System runs through the talk: wormholes, exotic matter, the Casimir effect, Toma's High Proximity joke, the LHC, ballasts, magnets and rare metals.
- Toma's notice: Euclid, next review 14 March 2030, no term for release. The surgeons in the valley pushed Pell's implant the rest of the way; it stays in. The Council signed level four the day Pryce was relieved, and Pell passed the 2316 image test.
- Last scene: Sigurrós lies in the snow under the open sky while Pell waits where her footprints end.

## Style

- Stage directions are in italics, third person, in US spelling, and describe only what a camera would see.
- Only the hyphen is used as a dash, and quotes are ASCII straight quotes.
- Foreign text gets a translation: under every paragraph in printed matter (`.gloss`), in brackets after the phrase in dialogue (`.tr`).
- Icelandic names keep their accents (Sigurrós Stefánsdóttir, Reykjavík) in narration, documents and Sigurrós's own speech. Other characters say them without accents: Sigurros, Reykjavik.
- Sedation is described plainly: she is put to sleep, falls asleep, wakes. Stage directions do not describe Sigurrós's voice in chapter 8 and later.
- Narration leaves out what a document's styling already shows (paper, ink, folds, sheets): letters and mail are drawn by CSS. Kellerman writes her letters on ripped A4 ruled paper. No fold lines are drawn on any paper.
- Device screens are drawn in CSS as their interface (coil, phone), never as pre; devices are never personified. Stage directions prefer the active voice. Perspective shifts use the SHIFT tag.
- Fixed sizes: screens, sheets and pages keep real proportions (A4 letters and mail, A5 notebook, 4:3 reader, phone); a setting switches them to fit their content. Papers grow and never break; screens scroll. Exceptions to the fixed sizes: envelopes and special formats, such as the Overseer Council leaflet in chapter 11, which is as tall as its text and shows no fold.
- "Quietly" is banned everywhere, in directions and in speech.
- A reply never gets relabeled. When a screen interrupts a reply, the rest of it is a continuation line (`+TAG:` in the source), drawn with no name.
- Low-level screens print ASCII only. Characters cite no forum as a source. Metric units only.
- Spoken numbers drop leading zeros: characters say 79, the page and screens print SCP-079.
- A jump in time starts a new chapter, with the interval stated in its opening scene.

## Markup

- **Chapter tabs:** `div.book-tabs[role=tablist]` at the top of `main`, one `button.tab.ctab` per chapter.
- **Chapter:** `article.chapter#cN`, which contains `header.ch-head > h2` and `div.ch-tabs[role=tablist]`, followed by its sections.
- **Section:** `section.sec#cN-sM`, which contains `h3 > span.sec-n + title + button.plink`.
- **Scene narration:** `div.scene > p`. Narration that comes after the conversation in a section has started keeps the same style as `div.scene.scene-mid`, with the smaller spacing step `--xs` (0.5rem) above and below it.
- **Reply:** `div.ln[data-sp=t|k|b|m|v|s|o|p] > b.sp + p`. The name column is as wide as the longest speaker name, measured by `js/names.js`, multiplied by 1.05 and capped at 5.4 rem into `--sp-w`. A continuation of the same reply after a screen is `div.ln.ln-cont` with no `b.sp`. Her label is `b.sp.sp-switch`: a click or Enter switches every one of her labels between SCP-239 and Sigurrós, and the choice is kept. Stage directions are `i.act`. A turn that is only a direction gets `.ln-act`.
- **Site announcement:** `div.ln.ln-pa`, a recording over the speakers, takes no name column and is not indented: it runs the full width of the line, under a small muted caption with a speaker mark and the words "Site announcement", a little smaller in the mono face.
- **Redaction:** `span.redact`.
- **The end:** the book's last scene is `div.scene.scene-final`, centered with no rule, and the last section closes with `div.the-end`, "The end" between two short rules.
- **Screens:** `details.app.app-<kind> > summary.app-cap + div.app-frame`. The kinds are `ghidra`, `ollama`, `konsole`, `vm` (virt-manager with a GNOME guest), and `079`.
- **Whiteboard:** `details.app.app-board`, black marker for the rules, blue for Cicero, red for the modern rhetoric, with the exception drawn as an SVG of dots. No watermark.
- **Translations:** a translation in brackets after foreign words in dialogue is `span.tr`.
- **Printed matter:** every attachment folds. The newspaper (`blockquote.paper`), the AKV poster (`div.ps-wrap > figure.poster`) and the hearing documents sit in `details.app.app-print` with a caption, like the screens. A folded attachment is only its caption, with no padding under it.
- **Hearing documents:** `blockquote.doc` inside `details.app.app-print`. `.doc-call` is a dark video-call tile, holding Sato's whiteboard (`.call-wb`) or Voss's shared screen (`.share`). `.doc-memo` is the Committee's decision on paper. `.doc-note` is Kellerman's notebook: an A5 page (148 mm wide, the pinout page at least 210 mm tall) of paper ruled every 7 mm, with a red margin and one ruled line of space above and below the writing. Everything on it is one hand: Caveat at one size, weight and color, headings underlined, every line written in the middle of the space between two rules. The ruling is an SVG tile, so the page uses no gradients. Sato's whiteboard is seen through her camera with the room around it: wall, the board in its frame with the marker tray, and the desk edge. The writing is one size, the same as on the conference room 3 whiteboard, and the board scrolls sideways on narrow screens. Numbers in tables are left-aligned with their headers. The last child of every block in these documents has no bottom margin.

## Interface

- **Settings:** the Settings button in the masthead shows or hides one row of controls under the title. The row keeps to one line and scrolls sideways when it is wider than the window. In Firefox the scrollbar under it shows while the pointer or keyboard focus is on the row. Its left or right edge fades out while there is more of the row that way; the fades sit on a wrapper, `div.mast-tools-wrap#settings`, and stop above the scrollbar so it stays usable, and in every browser the mouse wheel over the row scrolls it sideways until it reaches an end, then scrolls the page. The row is open by default, and its state follows the storage setting. Without JavaScript the page carries a `no-js` class, and the Settings button, the settings row, the key hints and the section link buttons are hidden, since none of them would work.
- **Keys:** n next section, p previous, b go to the bookmark (only in notes mode), s back to the top of the page, o pulls up the settings (with the masthead in view the row opens or closes in place; further down it comes down as a bar over the top of the window without moving the page; an open row leaves a placeholder of its exact height in the masthead, so nothing on the page shifts, and o, the gesture or Escape puts it away). On touch screens a quick tap with two fingers does the same as o, and the contents say so. Keys are ignored while typing and inside the Ghidra listing.
- **Bookmark:** part of the notes mode, so it shows only while highlights and notes are on. One per reader, marking where they left. Every reply's toolbar has a bookmark button. Setting a bookmark on another reply moves it, and the button on the bookmarked reply removes it. The bookmarked reply carries an orange ribbon on the divider above it. Go to bookmark, first in the settings row, or the b key, opens its section (also in tabbed view) and centers the reply. With notes off, the button, the ribbon, Go to bookmark, the b key and its hint are hidden or inactive, and the bookmark is kept. It follows the storage setting.
- **Tabbed view:** chapters become a row of tabs at the top of the story, and each chapter shows its sections as a second row of tabs. Neither row is sticky. Only one section of one chapter is on the page at a time, with previous and next buttons that cross chapter boundaries. Arrow keys, Home and End move between tabs.
- **Storage:** settings, highlights and notes are kept in sessionStorage and end with the tab. The Remember settings button moves them to localStorage and back. Settings that older versions saved permanently are cleared unless Remember settings is on.
- **Highlights, notes and quotes:** off by default, switched on with the Highlights and notes button. Off hides every button, note, mark, the bookmark and the panel without deleting anything. Each reply gets a floating toolbar at its top right corner, sitting on the divider above it in the style of GitHub's comment actions, so it takes no layout space. It appears after a deliberate hover (0.4 s), on keyboard focus, or on a tap on touch screens. The highlighter highlights the reply at once in its speaker's color and opens a row of other colors (the speaker's, yellow, orange, green, blue, pink) to change it. On a highlighted reply the highlighter removes the highlight, and so does the color already in use. The note button opens a text field under the reply, and a note mark appears before the speaker's name. Quotes are saved only from a selection: selecting words inside a reply shows a Quote button just above them, which saves exactly those words. The panel on the right, `details.notes-panel`, lists Highlights, Notes and Quotes in separate sections, with a count and a Clear all button. Switching the feature on opens the panel. While the feature is on, the right margin keeps room for the panel. Below 1200 px the panel sits above the story. Reply ids are `cN-sM-lK` (the Kth reply in section M).
- **Toggles:** Tabbed view, Hide contents, Hide stage directions, Stop blinking, Pretty wrap (on by default), Reading progress (the thin bar at the top of the window, on by default), Highlights and notes (off by default), Remember settings, the Theme control (Dark, Light or System, which follows the operating system), the Motion control (System follows the operating system's reduced-motion setting, Reduced turns smooth scrolling, transitions and animations off, Full keeps them on even when the system asks for less). Scrolling from links, keys and the bookmark is animated by `js/motion.js` frame by frame, at a steady speed with a short even slow-down over the last fifth, since browsers drop their own smooth scrolling when the operating system asks for less motion. While the page glides to a section, the contents glide straight to its link and skip the sections passed on the way. A wheel or touch stops the glide. The Scroll speed control (Slow, Normal, Fast) sets how long a glide takes. Jumping to a section leaves the pretty wrap script alone, and the script waits for a glide to finish before it measures the page and the page width control.
- **Page width:** a bordered control with its own label cell. Minus and plus change the width in steps of 4 rem, and Full removes the limit. The range is 40 to 120 rem and the default is 76 rem. Every choice follows the storage setting.
- **Layout:** the reading column is centered when the window allows. On large screens, from 1200 px, the contents take a fixed slot in the left margin, and a column wider than the remaining room grows to the right. Hiding the contents frees the slot, so the column re-centers or expands into it. Full width fills the window beside the contents, or the whole window when the contents are hidden. Below 1200 px the contents sit above the story. From 1200 px the contents and the notes panel scroll on their own with no scrollbar, and their top or bottom edge fades out while there is more to scroll that way. As the reader scrolls the story, the contents scroll to keep the current section's link near the middle. They move at a steady speed, and faster when the reader scrolls faster, so they keep the reader's pace. The contents keep their scroll position across a reload (saved like the other settings), and while the page settles after loading they are placed at once with no animation.
- **Pretty wrap:** native `text-wrap-style: pretty` on every element where supported. Elsewhere `js/pretty.js` visits every block of wrapping text on the page, finds a last line shorter than 15% of the block and joins the last words with no-break spaces: at most three words, and never so many that the line before falls below 75% of the width. Firefox uses the script. Add `?pw=js` to the URL to force the script.
- **Ghidra:**
  - Click a listing line or use the arrow keys to move the cursor. The status bar follows.
  - The Symbol Tree and the bookmark address navigate the listing.
  - Panes close with their X button and reopen from the Window menu.
- **Themes:**
  - The page is dark by default.
  - Ghidra, Ollama, Konsole and the virt-manager host window use dark themes, because the host desktop is KDE.
  - The GNOME guest and its windows are light.
  - The newspaper and poster are always paper-colored.
- **Contents button:** below 1200 px the contents open from a button in the bottom corner, styled like the settings buttons. The arrow tab on its left slides the whole button to the right at its own size until only the tab is left at the screen edge, and the tab slides it back. The tab owns the shared edge, so a hover lights every side of the part under the pointer. The choice is kept.
- **Calculator:** `div.calc` with a pixel display: an SVG of 216 by 144 dots, a high density, built by `pixlcd.py` from a 10 by 20 bitmap font (the X11 koi10x20 font, used at build time only, with θ made from its O and ∫ and the superscript minus one drawn by hand), graphs plotted dot by dot and joined the way the calculator joins points, with a pen two dots wide, on plain glass: the dots of characters show one by one with a thin gap round each, the way a dot matrix shows typed text, and plotted graphs are solid. The catalog's Play key moves the list a row at a time and plays even when motion is reduced. The case carries the invented brand Qalculator (after Qalculate!). Under the display a row of screen keys (Y=, WINDOW, ZOOM, TRACE, GRAPH) is always there; below it the full keypad, five columns with the arrow pad beside the first two rows and each key's second function in blue above it, can be hidden with Hide keys on the case above the display, on every calculator at once, and the choice is kept.
- **Animations:** the settings row slides open and shut in the masthead, the floating settings bar slides down from the top edge and back up, the floating contents pull out upward from their button like a drawer and slide back, the keypads of every calculator open and shut together like drawers, with the calculator under the pointer held in place, and folded screens and documents open and fold like a drawer: the browser keeps a details element's content in its own box, `::details-content`, hidden with content-visibility while shut, made a one-row grid whose row runs from 0fr to 1fr, with content-visibility waiting through allow-discrete, clipped vertically only. CSS alone, in every current browser. Nothing fades. Drawers, keypads, the contents menu and tab and the floating settings bar start fast and settle (cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 0.2, 1)); the settings row in the masthead opens and shuts at a linear pace, a click mid-slide carries on from the height already reached, and scroll anchoring is off while it moves, so quick clicks never move the page; the button and the o key toggle from the state last asked for, a held o counts once, and the floating bar caught mid-slide turns round where it is and keeps its single placeholder; the space under a caption closes with its drawer; the content keeps its own height while the row around it opens, so it is not laid out again on each frame; screens are measured only after a drawer has stopped.
- **Screens: always open:** a setting that opens every screen and document, hides the caption arrows and stops captions from folding them; turned off, each one goes back to how it was. All of them follow the Motion setting.
- **Folded screens:** a reply carried on after a screen keeps its name hidden; when the screen before it is folded, the name and the rule above it come back.
- **Pages outside the story:** the concept page, the summary and the glossary open with a row of tabs (Story, Concept, Summary, Glossary), the current page filled. Their section headings stand between the sections, with no margin of their own, so the sections' margins space them evenly above and below; the parts of a list entry share one small space under each, and the last entry of a list has no rule under it. In the glossary the first term of a letter has no space above and the last none below, so every gap between a heading and its terms is the sections' 16 px.
- **Toast:** short confirmations appear in a panel in the page's own theme colors.
- **CSS values:** every value repeated in a stylesheet is defined once in a `:root` block at the top of that file and used by name: colors by role (`--ghidra-select`, `--akv-blue`, `--hl-green`), lengths and times by value (`--l-0-6rem`, `--t-0-12s`). Media query breakpoints stay literal, because custom properties can't be used inside media queries.
- **Breakpoints:** the Material Design window size classes: compact below 600 px, medium 600 to 839 px, expanded 840 to 1199 px, large 1200 to 1599 px, extra-large from 1600 px. The three-column layout starts at large, the wider gutter at expanded, and phone layouts apply below 600 px.
- **Style limits:** no gradients, except the edge fade on the contents and the notes panel, which is a mask, and vertical spacing is equal above and below each block (`--s`). Section headings carry no margin, since the chapters read as one continuous text.
