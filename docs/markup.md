# Keep the Life: Markup

- **Chapter tabs:** `div.book-tabs[role=tablist]` at the top of `main`, one `button.tab.ctab` per chapter.
- **Chapter:** `article.chapter#cN`, which contains `header.ch-head > h2` and `div.ch-tabs[role=tablist]`, followed by its sections.
- **Section:** `section.sec#cN-sM`, which contains `h3 > span.sec-n + title + button.plink`.
- **Scene narration:** `div.scene > p`. Narration that comes after the conversation in a section has started keeps the same style as `div.scene.scene-mid`, with the smaller spacing step `--xs` (0.5rem) above and below it.
- **Reply:** `div.ln[data-sp=t|k|b|m|v|s|o|p] > b.sp + p`. The name column is as wide as the longest speaker name, measured by `js/text/names.js`, multiplied by 1.05 and capped at 5.4 rem into `--sp-w`. A continuation of the same reply after a screen is `div.ln.ln-cont` with no `b.sp`. Her label is `b.sp.sp-switch`: a click or Enter switches every one of her labels between SCP-239 and Sigurrós, and the choice is kept. Stage directions are `i.act`. A turn that is only a direction gets `.ln-act`.
- **Site announcement:** `div.ln.ln-pa`, a recording over the speakers, takes no name column and is not indented: it runs the full width of the line, under a small muted caption with a speaker mark and the words "Site announcement", a little smaller in the mono face.
- **Redaction:** `span.redact` holding its bars in a `span` with `aria-hidden="true"` and the word a screen reader says in `span.vh`: `<span class="redact"><span aria-hidden="true">████</span><span class="vh">redacted</span></span>`. The Redactions setting changes that word.
- **The end:** the book's last scene is `div.scene.scene-final`, centered with no rule, and the last section closes with `div.the-end`, "The end" between two short rules.
- **Screens:** `details.app.app-<kind> > summary.app-cap + div.app-frame`. The kinds are `ghidra`, `ollama`, `konsole`, `vm` (virt-manager with a GNOME guest), `079`, and in part 2 `ssw`, the secure workstation: `div.ssw` holds lines (`p.ssw-l`), numbered steps (`ol.ssw-steps`), key and value pairs (`dl.ssw-kv`), tables in a scrolling wrapper (`div.ssw-tw > table.ssw-t`, the transcript `.ssw-tr` with notes in `.ssw-tn`) and the key bar (`p.ssw-keys`); `div.ttx` is a teletext page and `div.dlg-screen > div.dlg` the firmware dialog. No screen uses `pre`, and no line is wrapped by hand.
- **Whiteboard:** `details.app.app-board`, black marker for the rules, blue for Cicero, red for the modern rhetoric, with the exception drawn as an SVG of dots. No watermark.
- **Translations:** a translation in brackets after foreign words in dialogue is `span.tr`.
- **Printed matter:** every attachment folds. The newspaper (`blockquote.paper`), the AKV poster (`div.ps-wrap > figure.poster`) and the hearing documents sit in `details.app.app-print` with a caption, like the screens. A folded attachment is only its caption, with no padding under it.
- **Documents and screens with a translation:** a document or a message read in another language is drawn as its medium, a screen in `details.app.app-print`: the court's ruling as paper (`blockquote.doc.doc-memo.doc-ruling`, with `.dr-parties`, `.dr-object`, `.dr-lead` and `.dr-sign`), the father's email as Toma's phone open in Thunderbird (`.phone` with `.pm`: `.pm-bar`, `.pm-subj`, `.pm-from`, `.pm-text`, `.pm-att`). Each line keeps its English translation directly under it as `span.tr.tr-line`, so the original and its translation form one block, and anything that shows or hides `.tr` treats them alike. A reply continued after such a screen is `div.ln.ln-cont` (in the build, a speaker code starting with `+`).
- **Scene shift in part 2:** narration that moves to another place and other people is `div.scene.scene-shift`, with the dashed rule (in the build, an item `"shift"`).
- **Grouped speakers:** a speaker written `"o5:O5-1"` in the build takes the color code `o5` and the name O5-1; `data-group="Overseers"` makes the making page count them together.
- **Hearing documents:** `blockquote.doc` inside `details.app.app-print`. `.doc-call` is a dark video-call tile, holding Sato's whiteboard (`.call-wb`) or Voss's shared screen (`.share`). `.doc-memo` is the Committee's decision on paper. `.doc-note` is Kellerman's notebook: an A5 page (148 mm wide, the pinout page at least 210 mm tall) of paper ruled every 7 mm, with a red margin and one ruled line of space above and below the writing. Everything on it is one hand: Caveat at one size, weight and color, headings underlined, every line written in the middle of the space between two rules. The ruling is an SVG tile, so the page uses no gradients. Sato's whiteboard is seen through her camera with the room around it: wall, the board in its frame with the marker tray, and the desk edge. The writing is one size, the same as on the conference room 3 whiteboard, and the board scrolls sideways on narrow screens. Numbers in tables are left-aligned with their headers. The last child of every block in these documents has no bottom margin.
- **Parts:** part 1 is index.html, part 2 is part2.html and part 3 is part3.html. Part 1 ends with `div.the-end` reading "End of part 1" and `p.part-next`, a link to part 2 drawn as a tool button, and part 2 ends the same way with "End of part 2" and a link to part 3. The ids of parts 2 and 3 carry the part (`article.chapter#p2c1`, `section.sec#p2c1-s1`, replies `p2c1-s1-l1`; `p3c1`, `p3c1-s1`), so highlights and notes kept for one part never match a reply in another. Each later part's masthead has a button to the part before it, `a.tool.mast-back`, before the Settings button.
- **Post-mortem cards:** part 3 is prose with no replies. Its first section holds `div.coda`, a row of `div.coda-card#p3c1-s1-pN` that wraps (each at least 18 rem wide and growing to fill the row), each a bordered card with a mono label (`p.coda-k`) over one paragraph, then the last line in `div.scene.scene-final`.
- **Articles in part 3:** every article is a section of its own after the cards (`p3c1-s2` and on, in the build a list `ARTICLES`), shown open with no fold: `div.web`, a web page with `header.web-bar` (`span.web-logo`, `span.web-nav`) and `div.web-body` holding `h4.web-title`, the byline `div.web-by` (`span.web-av` with the author's initial, then `p.web-by-t` with `span.web-name`, `span.web-role` and `span.web-date`), `p.web-lead`, then paragraphs under `h5` subheadings and a numbered list `ol.web-list`. Each line in another language keeps its translation under it in `span.tr.tr-line`, and the article has its own language switch, `span.web-lang` in the bar with three buttons, SK, EN and SK/EN, of which one is pressed at a time (`aria-pressed`, SK/EN by default), setting `data-lang` on `div.web` to `sk`, `en` or `both`; the translation setting does not reach the article. A GitLab project page is `div.glab`, in GitLab's dark theme on Engrama's own GitLab, whose top bar carries the Engrama logo (`svg/engrama/logo.svg`): `header.glab-bar`, then `div.glab-main` with the project sidebar `nav.glab-side` and `div.glab-page`, which holds the breadcrumb and Star button (`div.glab-top`) and `div.glab-cols`: the project (`div.glab-head`, `div.glab-tools`, `div.glab-commit`, the file list `div.glab-tw > table.glab-files`, `article.glab-readme`) and `aside.glab-info` with the project information. Icons are linked files from `svg/gitlab/` (`img.glab-i`), as the Ghidra toolbar's are. The sidebar folds below 820 px of page width and the information column moves under the files below 640 px (container queries). Hollis's notebook is `div.nb-spread` holding two pages side by side, each `blockquote.doc.doc-note.nb-hollis`, the ruled page of Kellerman's notebook in her own hand (Kalam) and blue ink; Sigurrós's letter is one `blockquote.doc.doc-note.nb-sig`, in a child's hand (Schoolbell) and pencil grey, with `p.nb-date` at the right and `p.nb-sign`; the pages stack when the column is too narrow for both. A news article is `div.news`: `header.news-bar` (`span.news-name`, `span.news-nav`) and `div.news-body` with `p.news-kicker`, `h4.news-head`, `p.news-stand`, `p.news-by` and the paragraphs, with one `blockquote.news-pull`. A chat is `div.rc > div.rc-in`, a Rocket.Chat workspace seen from one member's account: the room list `nav.rc-side` and `div.rc-room` with `header.rc-head`, `div.rc-list` (`p.rc-day`, then `div.rc-msg` with `span.rc-av` and `div.rc-m`, whose `p.rc-who` holds the name and time) and `div.rc-compose`; icons are `img.rc-i` from `svg/rocketchat/`, and the room list folds below 700 px of page width. The EU declaration of conformity is `blockquote.doc.doc-memo.doc-ce`, the memo paper with `header.dm-head`, the numbered points of Annex IV in `ol.dm-list`, each under a short heading (`p.dce-h`) with its facts as label and value rows (`dl.dce-kv`, labels in a 6.5 rem column, stacked below 560 px) or short lists (`ul.dce-ul`), and the signature in `p.dce-sign`. A lawyer's letter is `blockquote.doc.doc-memo.doc-lawyer` (as tall as its text, `header.dl-head` with `p.dl-from` and `p.dl-date`, then `p.dl-to`, `p.dl-re`, the paragraphs and `p.dl-sign`). The employment contract is `blockquote.doc.doc-memo.doc-cim`, its sections by letter in `ol.dm-list`, each Romanian line followed by `span.tr.tr-line`, and the two signatures in `div.cim-sign` (`span.cim-line`, Toma's in `span.cim-hand`). The Official Journal is `div.oj` (`header.oj-head` with `span.oj-lang`, `p.oj-ref`, `div.oj-body` with `p.oj-title`, `p.oj-pre` and `div.oj-arts`, one `section.oj-a` per article with `h5` reading "Article N: Title" on the left and the text on the right, numbered paragraphs in `ol.oj-num` and lettered points in `ol.oj-alpha`, stacked below 560 px of page width, and `p.oj-eli`). The GitLab releases page is `div.glab.glab-rels` with `div.glab-mile` (`div.glab-bar-p`) and one `article.glab-rel` per release (`span.glab-badge` for a pre-release), and an issue as `article.glab-issue` (`span.glab-open`, `p.glab-issue-meta`, one `div.glab-note` per comment). The postcard is `div.pc` (a group named for screen readers, up to 48rem wide) holding `div.pc-card` with `div.pc-front` (the picture and `span.pc-cap`) and `div.pc-back` (`div.pc-msg` in Kellerman's hand, `div.pc-side` with `span.pc-mark`, `span.pc-stamp` and `p.pc-addr`), then `button.pc-turn`. js/reading/postcard.js adds `pc-js` and `data-side` (front or back): both sides share one grid cell, and the card turns over in 3D with the button only, so the text on the card can be selected; the side turned away is `inert` and `aria-hidden`, the button names the side it turns to, and focus stays on it. With reduced motion the card changes side with no animation. Without the script the button stays hidden and both sides show one under the other. The lettering grows with the card (container units); below 34rem of card width the address goes under the message, the card grows as tall as the writing, and the picture keeps its whole width between the sky and the lake.
- **Translation switch:** the settings button `#t-gloss` (js/reading/glosses.js) cycles three modes: translations shown (normal), translations hidden (`body.gloss-original`), and translations only (`body.gloss-translation`), where the foreign words a translation covers (`.tr-orig`, marked by the script) and its brackets (`.tr-p`) are hidden and the translation takes the look of the text around it. In the two other modes every reply, scene or screen holding a translation shows a button, "Show original and translation", that brings the normal mode back and keeps the reply in place, like a reply hidden with the stage directions. The Engrama article (`div.web`) and the employment contract (`blockquote.doc-cim`) carry the class `lang-own` and their own switch, `.lang-sw` with three buttons (SK, EN, SK/EN in `span.web-lang`; RO, EN, RO/EN in `span.cim-lang`, at the top right of the paper in `div.cim-top`), one pressed at a time, setting `data-lang` on the container; the setting and its restore button do not reach them.
- **Time shift:** narration that jumps forward in time with the same people is `div.scene.scene-time`, with a dotted rule (in the part 2 build, an item `"time"`); a cut to another place stays `scene-shift`, dashed.
- **Contacts index:** the contacts app lists the contacts under letter headings and also draws an index on the right edge (`ol.ph-az`), as in Fossify Contacts: a star for the starred contacts, then only the letters that have contacts, the current one in a round mark. A tap jumps to the letter (js/devices/contacts.js). Starred contacts (Houston, Mama, Tata) head the list under the star and carry a gold star (`.ph-star`).
- **Source formatting:** every block element that holds only other block elements opens and closes on its own line, indented two spaces per level; an element holding text or inline elements stays on one line. Elements whose whitespace is shown (`pre`, `.gl`, `.gd`, `.k-screen`, `.ge-l`, `.fd-s`, `.cs-log`) are left as written. Typographic spaces are written as entities (`&nbsp;` before French colons and percent signs, `&#8239;` in French thousands), never as raw characters.
