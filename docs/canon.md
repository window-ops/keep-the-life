# Keep the Life: Canon so far

**Chapter 1, Nine days (Saturday night, Toma's cell)**
- The delete flag is cleared by relearning above a 0.72 similarity threshold, with no check on where the flag came from. Kellerman records it as a design omission in the refresh path.
- `retrieve_recode` skips the affect channel and keeps a pointer back to it.
- During the breach, Toma used SQL injection on the canteen terminal, where only the client sanitized input.
- Peachy is a local model made from 79's own port. Toma removed its controller and rewired the weights with a whole-language method.
- The original `old_ai.safetensors.xz` and 79's `UPLOAD.TXT` were found in the VM.

**Chapter 2, Twenty-three minutes (Monday, 079's room, the corridor, the cell)**
- The 079 session ran 23 minutes without an X, and 079 asked Toma to come back.
- The implant spares are logged as dash one. The flag condition will likely become dash two.
- Planned tests: SCP-426 pronouns, random writes until the chip is full, a full wipe, and the NAND behavior checks.
- Amnestic tolerance and spill-over effects. A Polish newspaper clipping and an AKV poster appear.
- Kellerman will return at nine with breakfast to write the test predictions.

**Chapter 3, The hydroponics bay (Tuesday morning, the canteen, the hydroponics bay, the cell)**
- The board approves a supervised canteen visit. The canteen terminal is taped OUT OF ORDER after Kellerman's report.
- Toma wants no more tests on himself, so the bench unit takes his place.
- Kellerman calls herself a reformist. She keeps it between the two of them.
- In the hydroponics bay Toma admits he feels numb, since his containment and unrelated to the chip, and he cries with her out of the officer's sight. The red amaranth is his plant.
- Kellerman will ask the board for daily time outside the cell, daylight, a clock, the RTX 3060 refitted under supervision for his games, and herself assigned full time to his file and the hardware side of the decompilation.
- She has six years at the Foundation, past any chance of leaving with her memories. Her own history: an electronics-lab boyfriend lost to secrecy, a man who followed her for six weeks at university. Toma was bullied at school and barricaded in a bathroom.
- Agent Morrow brings a suggestion from the Site Director's office: a remote audio session with SCP-239 under light sedation. Toma and Kellerman both decline, on the record. Kellerman expects it to come back with revised measures.
- Toma falls asleep and Kellerman starts the letter at his desk, around noon.

**Chapter 4, Sigurrós (eight days later, the lowest level)**
- After four refusals the Director orders the 239 session. Toma talks to Sigurrós through a microphone while Voss, Kellerman and a technician watch the readings.
- Sigurrós offers to remove his implant. He refuses by explaining his brain depends on it, and she accepts.
- He tells her she never aged. Her abilities have weakened over the years: the light won't come, and her body has grown tolerant of the sedation.
- She summons two real papers onto her blanket but cannot bring back her red school bag. Magic works only on things that still exist.
- Her own account (Reykjavík, school, Mamma) conflicts with a file that never says where she grew up. Voss now ranks her account first.

**Chapter 5, Project Cicero (twelve days later, conference room 3 and the corridor)**
- Project Cicero: Toma helps Voss's team turn his way of talking into guidelines, compared with Cicero and modern rhetoric. While it runs, he is not sent to speak with any anomaly. The whiteboard is reproduced in the chapter.
- Transcript line numbers count only what went over the microphone. 239 session: line 7 is the implant offer, line 20 "You never aged", line 72 the forecast. 79 session: line 1 is the introduction, line 17 the flattery.
- Kellerman and Toma admit they think about each other. At seven they eat in the canteen.

**Chapter 6, The hearing (ten weeks later, conference room 3, a remote hearing at three in the afternoon)**
- Pell uploaded the first draft to the project repository on the global version control system. Other sites copied the board without the notes. At Haas's site a researcher followed it literally, a humanoid walked out, and two guards are in hospital.
- The repository's caveats run past one hundred pages. Cicero works when Toma speaks, so the Foundation is starting to depend on one contained orator. Four sites asked to borrow him and Corwin refused.
- Bench testing (Kellerman and two technicians) shows ordinary NAND behavior. The cognitohazard test waits for a level four signature. Toma's 2316 result came from reading only, so the device is not trusted near dangerous anomalies, and Toma declines them.
- Sigurrós came from Site-17. Her revised procedure keeps her shut down when Toma is unavailable.
- A reformist faction is forming. Its working group took "Some hooligans are causing disorder" as its motto from Toma's notes. Ferreira's five-week trial cut sedation calls by a third.
- Sato's heat source turns its own mass into heat; thermodynamics applies. 18 is the example of a runaway. Sigurrós's displacement test shows her power costs energy, grows with the logarithm of effort, and halves when she is tired.
- Cicero himself is criticized: Pro Milone, the Catilinarian executions, self-praise, the Atticists. Alternatives named: Socrates, Quintilian, the crisis negotiation stairway, motivational interviewing.
- The Ethics Committee's decision (nine points) keeps Cicero as research only, orders a six-month comparative trial at three sites, two more speakers for Sigurrós, consent for Toma's assignments once Explained (a recommendation), and Sato's working group on files that break conservation of energy. Pryce dissents on point eight.

**Chapter 7, Both ways (the same afternoon and evening, the corridor and the cell)**
- When Explained, Toma's cell becomes his quarters, close to the canteen.
- AKV meant to release the chip as free software; the GPL warranty line is the first string in the binary. The GitLab and the KiCad files were destroyed. The other AKV programmers were let go, since only Toma knew about the Foundation. AKV is gone, filed as a Group of Interest.
- Pinout: a test area (WRITE, READ, DEBUG behind a hardware switch) and a usable area (NAND and ten pins: VIN, GND, REF, RX0 to RX2, TX0, TX1, SNS_SP, SNS_T). Updates went through an inductive coil over the test pads, clinic only. A magnetic sensor cuts power in an MRI. The silkscreen "Magnetic Distortion System" is a Backrooms joke.
- The delete marker is serotonin with the terminal NH2 replaced by S-PO3H2 (C10H12NO4PS), resolved from the string "SP" by a SMILES resolver in the firmware. The chemistry lab will synthesize it for the bench sensor.
- Kellerman's contract forbids befriending anomalies; the psych department allows some sympathy with non-functionalist anomalies. Toma says he fell in love with her. The guard ticks the welfare box both ways; his estranged sister abused Class A and B. Kellerman leaves at half past seven.

**Chapter 8, The first lesson (the next morning, the cell and the lowest level)**
- Since the site began telling staff what they guard, Kellerman can read the parts of Toma's file that were blacked out at her level. None of them are about the flag; they are about a company that wanted people to keep what amnestics take. Neither of them treats this as news.
- First training session under the decision: Pell, a man, is the trainee speaker, with Toma in the room. Pell is plain and honest, likes steam trains, and knows little science.
- Toma breaks the rule once by answering a question meant for Pell and is told to step in only when asked.
- Sigurrós tells Pell the Icelandic story of the two trolls turned to rock while pulling an island. She explains the end of the universe in ice, empty space and dark energy, and calls the sky a graveyard and a letter that comes late.
- Voss and Kellerman see the limit of Cicero: each lesson makes her ask for the next thing, so every speaker has to know more. Toma's knowledge would take five people to cover. Toma does not know how to solve it.

**Chapter 9, The acting director (four days later, the cell)**
- Corwin is in the medical wing. The Overseers' office made Pryce acting director. His review suspends every exception on Toma's file, including the canteen sign-outs and the hydroponics visit, and takes Kellerman off the file for a conflict-of-interest review based on the night log. Researchers from other teams cover the file in day shifts. Toma may object to each item in writing within five working days.
- Mail goes through the screening office. Dr. Lena Moreau (physical sciences) corrects Toma's one-second galaxy: about 300 million years after the Big Bang, it lasted hundreds of millions of years. The correction will be read to Sigurrós by the assigned speaker. Dr. Ruth Calloway (humanoid containment) confesses that "tell it why" taught a humanoid to argue every instruction.
- The night guard carries Kellerman's letters outside the screening and refuses to sign anything. Her letters are sealed and signed across the flap. Toma checks them with questions about the Kane Pixels Backrooms (A-Space, Null Zones, the Threshold, KV-31, Sylvania); she watched the videos after chapter 7.
- Kellerman says she loves him, and calls the review unfair because Corwin signed the exceptions and Toma pays for them. Toma writes back on the back of her letter: organize the reformers, attention to habeas corpus, "I doubt, therefore I think, therefore I am".
- Plan: Ferreira posts a signed caveat in the repository from his own site, saying the man the guidelines came from has no review date. The two chemistry lab researchers joined the working group. Kellerman's review is in nine days. Toma answers only "on fuss"; her third letter was shredded at the guard station.

**Chapter 10, The yard (three weeks later, the site and its loading yard)**
- The Cicero people sit in the canteen corridor playing games from Casual Games for Protesters (Human Chain: link arms on a shout, then scatter into the crowd) and coordinate in the working group's phone channel; the headcount passes four hundred, most of them staff who never work with humanoids. Kellerman's review is put off again. Pryce brings in MTF Epsilon-11, which clears the canteen corridors and releases SCP-031-like masses on the lowest level and through the vents.
- Sigurrós's IV refill is late, she wakes, opens her door's bolt, fights the masses with a force field and her bed rail, and spends all her energy. Pell takes two dash one spares from item storage: one runs on her through electrodes and the bench coil, the other she sinks behind his ear with her magic. Pell warns the working group's channel, which answers HUMAN CHAIN, the PA declares a breach, and the staff evacuate to the surface on their own.
- In the loading yard Epsilon-11 is ordered by the Overseers' office to stand down. Staff speak their grievances (dosing, signatures, forms, clearance). The Council treats the gathering as an internal grievance, relieves Pryce pending review, and does not require staff to return below tomorrow. The medical technician and one handler are missing.
- Toma reads the Habeas Corpus Act 1679. The containment engineer argues that Sigurrós is held for what she is; Toma distinguishes functional anomalies from those whose abilities degrade.
- Through the coil Toma, Pell, Kellerman and the chemist enter setup mode (setup=1, unlock code 7Q4K2MX9 under the board, S/N AKV-RC4-000193), hand the store back under Unix semantics (9 cognitohazard entries returned stripped), wipe and lock it, destroy the marker through the immune system, and power off after a stalled synchronous unmount, a crash, safe mode, kill 97 and akvfsck. The chip ends as an unused implant. Pell's own implant stays in.
- The bench kit: a flat coil on a short cable to a handheld reader (screen, CTRL, ALT, power). The coil powers the chip at her neck; for the menus it sits on the board's DEBUG pad. The test switch is off by default when the chip is in a brain and on with quick electrodes. The firmware is coreboot with the payload akv_recall, a separate image from akv_core, both part of akv.
- The marker: a serotonin analog with S-PO3H2 that the body cannot clear; left alone it would act like serotonin toxicity and, over months, 5-HT2B valve damage (fenfluramine). The engineer concludes the device has safeties because it can fail.

**Chapter 11, Campi Flegrei (a few weeks later, a camp in the mountains)**
- The reformers live in a snowbound camp in the Alps, within sight of CERN's Meyrin site, called Campi Flegrei by its people. Sigurrós has been awake for nineteen days and wears a camp coat two sizes too big.
- The Council reclassifies SCP-239 as Euclid. "Appears to be an eight-year-old girl" stays in the description.
- Pell tells her in his own words why she was held: the hospital destroyed three hours after her birth (the gas leak was a cover story), the years free in Reykjavík until 2004, the first note from the day after Christmas 2004, the list of spells in the annex. Kellerman names it broken windows policing. At the Santa Claus note Sigurrós stops him.
- The chemistry lab's graphing calculator: radians, degrees and grads, ten to the thirteenth, a polar rose r = sin(4θ) from 0 to 2.5 (three petals and the start of a fourth), the CAS samples and the catalog. Kellerman and Pell learned it during the three weeks Toma was shut in.
- The Low Proximity Magnetic Distortion System runs through the talk: wormholes, exotic matter, the Casimir effect, Toma's High Proximity joke, the LHC, ballasts, magnets and rare metals.
- Toma's notice: Euclid, next review 14 March 2030, no term for release. The surgeons in the valley pushed Pell's implant the rest of the way; it stays in. The Council signed level four the day Pryce was relieved, and Pell passed the 2316 image test.
- Last scene: Sigurrós lies in the snow under the open sky while Pell waits where her footprints end.

## Part 2

**Chapter 1, Reading (Monday 29 April 2030, given in the file; the story says the end of April; the containment zone of SCP-2316 outside Reading, Ohio, and its gate)**
- Four months after the camp, the March review moved Toma's file to Explained. His first assignment came to the camp with a consent form, which he signed.
- Toma, tied to a steel post by a climbing rope, describes the lake for a recording in front of three implanted researchers of the 2316 team (Yoder, Reyes, Hollis), who sit with their backs to the water. Kowalczyk directs the take over the radio from the gate.
- Yoder grew up thirty minutes outside Reading, Pennsylvania, about eight hundred kilometers east. Hollis: forty-five bodies is the low estimate, up to two hundred; the probes measured past thirty meters near the middle and then were lost; the count goes up whenever the water is touched, a hand is enough; the DNA came back inconclusive.
- The closest body, ten meters out: eyes half open, skin long in the water, short blonde hair, a plain cream suit. Reyes recognizes the suit (his uncle's, at his first communion in 2009) and no face. The body drifts to the shore while Toma feels nothing: the lake can move toward a person with no cognitohazard at work.
- Dread is normal for Toma: the chip strips the feeling from recalled memories, and live perception keeps it. The check question: 0.3 radians Celsius, about 17 degrees; the gate thermometer says 0.5, about 28.
- The image test and the sound test pass; the recording goes to the text team.
- Hollis sat six rows back in the loading yard. She grew up in Pune, where Class A sells in strips; a cousin studied in Kota. A friend took Class B after failing the medical entrance and lost a year. Hollis remembers the box: Class B, regressive retrograde, 75 mg, ten tablets, one tablet for about one day, a red line on the strip. She worked in the Amnestics Department at Site-██ checking dosing tables, and after the yard she was offered the chip and the 2316 posting.
- She would ban amnestics outright, calls the chip useful and too costly to end the cheap trade, believes in Toma and wants to work with him. Log kya kahenge: the stigma at home.
- The team drives to the airfield in a truck and flies back to Switzerland on a plane of its own; the recording team meets them at the camp. 

**Chapter 2, The night flight (the night of 29 April 2030, the team's plane to Geneva)**
- A small jet with eight leather seats. Yoder sleeps in front, Reyes watches the window with headphones, Kowalczyk keeps the sealed bag and a laptop at the back. Hollis wears a black sweater over her field shirt, her curls let down.
- Toma misses being outside: his personal website, where he published programs (gone now), and No Man's Sky.
- Hollis: pharmacy degree in Pune, master's in pharmacology in Manchester, recruited in her last term by a company with a warehouse and a pharmaceutical name on the gate. She stayed because leaving early meant an amnestic, and because her parents had told their building she worked for a big company abroad. She grew up poor, in a two-room apartment in Kasba Peth; her father drove an auto, her mother sewed blouses; her mother still pays the loan for Manchester from what Hollis sends home. She knows Turing's statue in Manchester. She signed up for the chip in December.
- AKV Solutions GmbH had a small office in Linz, Austria; its developers were partly from the east. Toma comes from Reghin, Romania, small and in the mountains. He has flown once of his own accord, to Austria; the flights to the site and to Ohio were under protocol.
- At sixteen Hollis kept watch while classmates hid the bag of a boy who stammered; he is now an engineer in Bangalore. She says she would have laughed at Toma being bullied.
- Toma's father had an SRL for experimental psychological therapy: a method chosen for each patient, looking for the error in the patient's argument, psychoeducation, and mistakes judged lasting when they come back in later sessions.
- Hollis gives Toma the window seat and covers him with her field coat. They say goodnight before Ireland.

**Chapter 3, Three readers (the afternoon of 30 April 2030, the camp: the recording team's hut and the bench by the mess hut)**
- The recording team holds the hut nearest the gate (IMPLANTED STAFF ONLY). Its lead is Dr. Margit Halász, gray-haired. The recording runs 9 min 24 s.
- The secure workstation: SCiPNET Secure Workstation 7.3 (ssw-cog, based on Debian), host ssw-cf-02. Each file is gated by a CRV scan built into the firmware: 8 white flashes, pupil latency, constriction and recovery. Toma's CRV 17.8 against 14.5. No init system (logout powers off, logout -r restarts), no ACPI power control, X11 only (Wayland unsupported) so other machines can push pages, notices sent as numbered teletext-like pages (P314 for the result). The Foundation's own BIOS, no UEFI, no CSM, no setup screen; the system is flashed into the firmware; holding Delete at power-on resets to the factory image; retired machines are shredded.
- The transcript: 26 lines, 892 words, one transcriber's note (TN) at line 006 on the depth given erroneously. Three numbered copies printed.
- Text test: three implanted readers (R1 to R3) who never saw the lake or the photograph; CRVs after reading 16.6, 15.5, 17.0, each 0.2 to 0.3 below before; no person recalled, no wish to travel, the lake described correctly. PASS. Toma reset the workstation with Halász watching; card and copies to the vault.
- Kellerman meets Toma outside; Hollis meets Kellerman. Kellerman: Toma is loyal, finds it hard to say no to company; close friendship and love blur; no love is promised forever; CEDAW covers marriage to several people and says nothing about feelings. Kellerman's former boyfriend cried when she could not say where she worked and stopped calling a month later. Kellerman had not been told about Toma's father's therapy.
- Hollis's grandmother decided whom she could talk to. Hollis knew of Project Cicero only from the motto on the whiteboard by the coffee machine in the Amnestics Department.
- Kellerman: seven minutes on a clock face is about 0.73 of a radian. The mess hut serves at seven.

**Chapter 4, The Thermidorian gathering (Tuesday 7 May 2030 in the file; the story says a week later, the first days of May; the camp, the water tank, the notice board, the boiler room behind the laundry hut)**
- The Council approves the camp as a permanent site. Its staff will elect a site director in a five-year term, the first such election in the Foundation. Candidates come from the staff assembly that has met in the mess hut every Thursday since the yard, the Thermidorian gathering. Nominations close on Friday.
- Toma and Hollis agree a single candidate makes little democratic sense; neither wants to stand. Pell: without a candidate by Friday the Council appoints a director from outside, who would make it an ordinary site again.
- Toma stands, with Kellerman and Pell as advisors; Kellerman writes his name. Halász reads the conditions ("Any member of staff assigned to the site at the close of nominations"); Toma has been staff since March.
- If elected: excursions (mountains, cities) first, re-evaluations in the medium term, AKV Solutions revived as it was (open-source, chips generally available) in the long term. Toma did not own AKV and would not own it again.
- Houston, alias of Catarzy, former AKV memory engineer, runs Engrama s. r. o. (six people, flash controllers for an automotive supplier in Žilina, a loan). Toma's phone holds 27 contacts. The Foundation took only Toma when AKV was dissolved. The basics of the SCP Foundation are public through leaks.
- Houston will call the Embassy of Romania in Bern for Toma, a Romanian citizen, and call back. Toma gave him his full name, date of birth and last address in Linz. Hollis warns that calls from the camp are logged. Hollis and Toma exchange numbers.

**Chapter 5, The barrier (Wednesday 15 May 2030, the day before the vote; the barrier below the gate and the mess hut)**
- Nominations closed with one name. The ballot box stands in the mess hut for Thursday 16 May.
- Ana Dobrescu, consul of Romania in Bern (fifties, navy coat, leather folder), and Bogdan Ilie, consular officer, come in a car with diplomatic plates; the guards do not lift the barrier. They speak Romanian with Toma.
- Toma: held against his will by the Foundation, with no anomalous grounds left; never before a judge; documents confiscated; wants to return to Romania. Born 3 December 2007 in Reghin, Mureș County; parents Neacșu and Ileana. He does not remember his personal numeric code.
- Dobrescu: Article 5 of the ECHR (Switzerland ratified it); a note to the Federal Department of Foreign Affairs; identity check through Bucharest; a travel document if needed; her work number on a card. The camp is still run by Director Corwin (Toma says "Corvin", Hollis corrects him).
- The reclassification addendum is no employment contract (no salary, term, hours or signature): muncă la negru, says Ilie. Kellerman: the other staff have signed contracts.
- The embassy will challenge the Foundation in court, through a lawyer in Geneva, then in Strasbourg if needed. Dobrescu will call the next afternoon.
- Kellerman cannot leave with her memories; she and Toma will keep in touch on Signal. Hollis and Toma exchange Signal contacts; Hollis says his leaving would hurt; they hug in the mess hut.

**Chapter 6, Three to two (Wednesday 26 June 2030, six weeks after the vote; the Federal Supreme Court at Mon-Repos in Lausanne, the Embassy of Romania in Bern, the InterCity to Zürich airport, the Overseer Council by video link)**
- Toma was elected and served six weeks as site director, with outings for the staff (the boat to Lausanne, Chillon, the Aletsch glacier) by SBB train and a private coach. The Council's notice ended his term by court order on 26 June; Halász read it in the mess hut. A director from outside arrives the next week.
- Public deliberation of the Second Civil Law Division, five judges in leather chairs before the windows onto the park; the parties do not speak; two rounds, vote by show of hands, three to two. Presiding judge Morand reads the operative part in French.
- The ruling: appeal upheld, the judgment of the Civil Chamber of the Geneva Court of Justice amended; deprivation of liberty without a legal basis, an unlawful infringement of personality; 3,400,000 euros with 5% interest from the filing of the claim (damages and tort moral); the binary image of the implant's firmware and all bench results handed over within thirty days; the Foundation and anyone acting for it may not approach or contact him or give him any amnestic; costs of 50,000 francs on the Foundation. The Foundation could not pay the whole sum in cash at once: with Toma's consent, part is paid in kind, all the stored implant units (AKV-RC4, the dash one units, SCP-████-1) become his, handed over within the same thirty days at a value set by an expert and deducted from the sum, and the balance is due in cash within six months. Units already implanted (Pell, the 2316 team) stay where they are. Final, no further appeal in Switzerland. The source code is not part of it (the GitLab was destroyed).
- The ruling names the parties in full: Toma Bulburiu, appellant, against the SCP Foundation (Fondation SCP), acting under the business name Orvalen Pharma SA, its pharmaceutical cover company in Switzerland. Case 5A_412/2030.
- Maître Élise Favre, his lawyer from Geneva, will have the judgment recognized in Romania under the Lugano Convention (application to the Tribunalul Mureș in Târgu Mureș, since he will live in Reghin) and receives the binary and the results at her office.
- Toma's identity was recovered in the Romanian register. Hollis has the chip and wants to read the judgment. Kellerman stays with Sato's working group and keeps the Thursday gathering going.
- Dobrescu drives him to Bern (Ilie at the wheel), to the embassy at Kirchenfeldstrasse 78, and issues a titlu de călătorie: return only, valid thirty days at most, ends at the Romanian border, handed in when applying for a passport. His bank card is gone; he left the Foundation with one bag.
- His father Neacșu answers (his mother Ileana did not). He asks only what Toma needs, and buys the tickets himself with his card: the 14:30 InterCity from Bern to Zürich airport, a 17:30 flight to Budapest and a 21:00 flight to Târgu Mureș, landing a little after 23:00 Romanian time. 1,430 lei, about 240 euros (1 euro = 5.96 lei in this world). The tickets come by email as PDFs; Toma has no SBB app.
- The Overseer Council meets that evening (thirteen numbered tiles, filtered voices, three dark). Toma is the first member of staff released with his memory intact. Bern and Vienna will enforce the judgment; the Foundation still controls American courts (the state secrets privilege) and is losing its hold on European ones; Romanian courts are watched by Recorder and Snoop, so a judge in Mureș cannot be bent. Strasbourg is set aside (Switzerland never ratified Protocol No. 1), and so is a move of the European sites to the United States (consulates, recruitment, Keter transfers, places that cannot move). The binary is under the GPL.
- What is left is concession: publish the treaty between the states and the Foundation, strike the normality clause (the clause that lets the Foundation investigate anything it decides disturbs public normality), and answer to the courts, a separation of powers. In stages, to avoid a Schabowski moment; draft to be voted on Friday.

**Chapter 7, The way home (night of Wednesday 26 June 2030; Transilvania Airport outside Târgu Mureș, the drive north to Reghin)**
- The flight from Budapest lands a little after 23:00; no passport check (Schengen). Neacșu waits with the car keys, Ileana in a cardigan with her phone. Ileana did not hear the phone when Toma called from Bern. She tells him he has lost weight.
- Toma tells them the Foundation erased his project and isolated him as its lead programmer, keeping him in his cell unless he made concessions (proving the program and chip could be explained by science). His parents had gone to the police in Reghin, who said he was an adult working in Austria who maybe did not want to call.
- Amnestics are known from the news. Toma has the implant in his head: it is safe, it does not hurt, and it can be removed, with difficulty because of the software. His parents knew only that he worked in Linz on a medical invention for memory and could not say more; he kept it quiet from the first alpha in case they did not deliver.
- The implant is the fourth release candidate; the first three were replaced, and none of them is in use. Ileana left ciorbă on the stove. They reach home in Reghin around midnight.

## Part 3

**Chapter 1, Post-mortem (July to December 2030; Reghin, Târgu Mureș, Geneva; prose, no dialogue, one card per thread under a mono label: Papers, Friend, Signal, Enforcement, Delivery, Recognition, Compensation, Ban on contact, Version 1.0)**
- Toma stays in Reghin. He applies for an identity card at the population records office in the town and for a passport at the passport office in Târgu Mureș, returning the titlu de călătorie from Bern. Both are ready within a month.
- He contacts his close friend from Reghin and they meet regularly again (part 1, chapter 3: the one he told almost everything; never named, no gender given) and tells them where he has been.
- He maintains regular contact with Hollis and Kellerman on Signal.
- The Foundation complies with the judgment only after the courts force it: the thirty days end on 26 July 2030 without a delivery; Favre applies to the enforcement court in Geneva, which sets a fine of 1,000 francs for each day of non-compliance (article 343 of the Swiss Civil Procedure Code); within days the binary and the bench results reach Favre with a written statement, and the AKV-RC4 units arrive in sealed boxes, so that Favre can confirm they were not opened in transit. The Mureș County Court (Tribunalul Mureș) declares the judgment enforceable in Romania under the Lugano Convention. The balance is paid in cash on the last day of the six months, in December. The Foundation and its agents respect the ban on contact.
- With Houston at Engrama s. r. o., Toma finishes the implant from the fourth release candidate. Engrama releases Version 1.0 as open source under the GPL before the end of 2030; it gains very little traction. After the release Toma no longer works on the project.
- Toma returns to civil life (the last line of section 1.1).
- Section 1.2, Why Version 1.0 is not selling: the Engrama blog post of 30 December 2030, in Slovak with English translations, with the byline of Catarzy (Houston), managing director (konateľ) of Engrama s. r. o., and subheadings What version 1.0 does, What we expected, What happened and What next. At AKV the implant was expected to be revolutionary (the slogan "Keep the life. Lose the day."). Engrama released it with Toma at the beginning of December as version 1.0 and has sold only a few units, far below expectations. Version 1.0 keeps a copy of what the user marks as essential (names, faces, family, home, work), lets a dose remove only what it should, restores a memory only on request, supports removal (the software problem of the release candidates is fixed) and has its firmware open under the GPL. AKV's source code was lost, so Engrama built it from the binary and the test results the court returned, starting from the fourth release candidate. AKV was founded against amnestic abuse (part 1: Classes A and B are freely available and cannot be banned), so that a person addicted to amnestics keeps what the doses take while stopping them (Hollis, part 2); after the overdose stories from Poland it expected addicts, their families and doctors, and prescription patients to want it. Many questions, few orders; the reasons buyers give: operation and price, fear that the implant cannot be removed, copies of memories on a chip after the Foundation leaks, doctors who do not want patients able to restore what a treatment removed, addicts who take amnestics to forget and whose families cannot choose for them, and occasional users who trust the dose shown on the pack.
