1.1The delete mark

Site-██ has its long-term humanoid cells on the lower east corridor, behind a pair of airlocked doors and a desk where the night guard signs every entry. One of them belongs to Toma, designated SCP-████, a former programmer at AKV Solutions whose anti-amnestic implant could never be fully removed, and who was downgraded from Keter to Euclid months ago. The cell has a bed, a shelf of borrowed books, a sink and his own recovered workstation, now air-gapped and bolted to the desk, its monitor filled with columns of disassembled instructions. The night log shows him awake for most of the last nine days, and at 03:10 on Saturday the guard flagged it to a researcher.

Dr. Iris Kellerman is twenty-eight, the newest researcher assigned to his file, and one of three staff cleared to enter the cell alone. Her copper-red hair falls on her shoulders in loose waves, parted in the middle, framing a pale face with dark brown eyes, thick dark brows, a thin line of eyeliner and lips in a muted red. She wears a loose white linen shirt open over a black top, her badge clipped to the pocket, and she carries a tray with a bowl of soup and a paper cup of tea from the staff canteen.

Kellerman

knocking twice on the open door frame and stepping inside The night log says you've been at that screen since six yesterday morning, Toma. setting the tray down on the far corner of the desk, clear of the keyboard, and looking at the columns of hex on the monitor What are you hunting for in there that can't wait until you've slept?

Toma

I need to identify a race condition in my program that caused it to be flagged as anomalous.

Kellerman

How does a program fault end up looking like that to us?

Toma

You are not supposed to know about 2316, because the very concept of it is a cognitohazard and access is restricted to four and upper. But the reason I can know about it without any active cognitohazard acting on me is because a lot of times those cognitohazards have to deal, a lot, with subjective perception. The device does not understand those concepts, and it will make sure that when you retrieve any dangerous information, you can understand the concept rationally, without having an opinion or feeling about it. It's essentially an equivalent to the Class E amnestic, the Ennui amnestic, designed to induce complacence with the anomalous.

Kellerman

What actually happens is that the original memories are recovered in full.

Kellerman

PHONE / WORMHOLE THEORIES

⌕ wormhole theories

Wormhole theories

  • Einstein-Rosen bridge (1935): the first wormhole found in Einstein's equations. It pinches shut faster than light could cross it, so nothing gets through.
  • Morris-Thorne wormhole (1988): a wormhole built to stay open long enough to cross. It needs exotic matter with negative energy to hold its throat apart.
  • Visser thin-shell wormholes (1989): the exotic matter is gathered into a thin shell around the throat, so less of it is needed.
  • Gao-Jafferis-Wall (2016): a quantum link between the two ends supplies the negative energy, and the wormhole opens for a short time.
  • Maldacena, Milekhin and Popov (2018): a wormhole wide enough for a person, in a model of the universe with an extra dimension. Crossing it would take about a second for the traveler.
  • Quantum simulation (2022): physicists sent a signal through a tiny wormhole model run on a quantum processor. It was a simulation, and therefore, no space was crossed.
