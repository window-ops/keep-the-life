A-Space

In the Backrooms series by Kane Pixels, the Async Research Institute's name for the Backrooms as a whole, which it hoped to turn into units of space for storage and housing.

Chapters: 7, 9

ACPI

Advanced Configuration and Power Interface, the standard since 1996 through which a PC's operating system tells the hardware to sleep or to switch itself off. Many modern ARM boards and embedded controllers have no ACPI: they power off through the processor's firmware interface (PSCI) or through a power management chip named in the device tree, and some can only stop and report that it is safe to cut the power. A shutdown that ends on such a message, with a switch or a person making the final cut, also suits systems where the last step should be a deliberate act. In the story, the implant reports when it is safe to shut down, and the recording team's workstation says when it is safe to switch off.

Chapter: 10; Part 2, chapter: 3

Alan Turing

The English mathematician who led codebreaking work at Bletchley Park in the Second World War and later worked on early computers at the University of Manchester. A memorial statue of him sits on a bench in Sackville Gardens, Manchester.

Part 2, chapter: 2

Aletsch Glacier

The largest glacier in the Alps, in the canton of Valais in Switzerland, about 23 kilometers long. It is part of the Jungfrau-Aletsch UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Alpha

An early version of a program, released for testing inside the team before its features are complete. A beta follows it, then the release candidates.

Part 2, chapter: 7

Amnestic

In the SCP universe, a drug that erases memories. In the story, Class A removes the last few hours and Class B reaches a few weeks back, stronger classes go further, and in some countries they are sold without prescription.

Chapters: 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 10; Part 2, chapters: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7

Amnestics Department

The Foundation department that develops amnestics and sets their doses. In the story, Hollis checked its dosing tables for field teams, from a single witness to a whole street, before she transferred to the SCP-2316 team.

Antiderivative

A function whose derivative is the given function. The antiderivative of x² is x³/3, plus any constant.

Apollyon

In the SCP universe, the object class for an anomaly that cannot be contained and is expected to break out.

Async Research Institute

The research company of the Backrooms series by Kane Pixels, which builds the Threshold and opens a doorway into the Backrooms in the late 1980s.

Auto rickshaw

A three-wheeled motorized taxi common in Indian cities, usually called an auto. Many drivers own their vehicle on a loan in their own name.

Backrooms

An internet legend of endless empty office rooms with yellow walls and buzzing lights. The story uses the version of the video series by Kane Pixels.

Chapters: 7, 9, 11

Ballast

The part of a fluorescent light that gives the tube a high voltage to start and then limits the current, which would otherwise keep rising until the tube burned out.

Chapter: 11

Bern

The federal city of Switzerland, seat of its government and of the foreign embassies, among them the Embassy of Romania.

BIOS

The firmware that starts a PC and hands it to the operating system, used from the early 1980s until UEFI replaced it. In the story, the Foundation builds its own BIOS for its secure workstations.

Part 2, chapter: 3

Bletchley Park

The country house in England where British codebreakers read German ciphers during the Second World War, among them the Enigma. Its staff kept their work secret for decades after the war.

Chapter: 3; Part 2, chapter: 2

Broken windows policing

A policing method from a 1982 article by James Q. Wilson and George Kelling, adopted in New York in the 1990s: minor offenses such as broken windows or fare evasion are punished so that serious crime does not follow.

Chapter: 11

C

Campi Flegrei

The Phlegraean Fields, from Greek for burning (καύση): a volcanic caldera west of Naples whose ground slowly rises and sinks, last erupted in 1538. In the story, the name the reformers give their camp in the Alps: campi means both fields and camps in Italian, and both places are hilly ground. The concept page has more.

Chapter: 11; Part 2, chapter: 3; Part 3, chapter: 1

Casimir effect

A small force between two metal plates placed very close together in a vacuum. The space between them holds slightly less energy than the space outside, one of the few sources of negative energy measured in a laboratory.

Chapter: 11

CEDAW

The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women, adopted by the United Nations in 1979. Its committee states that polygamous marriage contravenes a woman's right to equality.

Part 2, chapter: 3

Chaos Insurgency

In the SCP universe, a group that broke away from the Foundation and uses anomalies as weapons.

A medieval castle on a rock island on the shore of Lake Geneva, near Montreux in the canton of Vaud.

Part 2, chapter: 6; Part 3, chapter: 1

Cicero

Marcus Tullius Cicero (106 to 43 BC), Roman orator, lawyer and statesman, whose writings on rhetoric are still taught. Project Cicero in the story is named after him.

Chapters: 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10

Ciorbă

A Romanian sour soup, made with vegetables and often meat and soured with borș or lemon juice.

Part 2, chapter: 7

The personal numeric code, a 13-digit number given to every Romanian citizen at birth and printed on identity documents.

In the SCP-2316 file, a score a reader must reach before the file opens, checked by an automated verification. In the story, it is measured from the eyes before each file opens.

Cognitive therapy

A form of psychotherapy, developed by Aaron Beck in the 1960s, that looks for errors in the way a person interprets events and works with the person to correct them. It is the basis of cognitive behavioral therapy.

In the SCP universe, something that harms a person through the act of seeing, hearing or knowing it. The reader's screens call such an entry a harmful memory.

The law that energy is neither created nor destroyed, only changed from one form to another. It follows from the laws of physics staying the same over time.

Chapter: 6

A state's official in another country who looks after its citizens there: visits citizens who are detained, issues documents and helps them deal with local authorities.

Chapters: 1, 3

Degree, radian and grad

Three ways to divide a full turn: 360 degrees, about 6.28 radians (two pi), or 400 grads. One radian is the angle at which the arc along the circle is as long as its radius, about 57.3 degrees.

Latin for to teach, to delight and to move: the three duties of an orator according to Cicero.

Einstein-Rosen bridge

The first wormhole found in the equations of general relativity, in 1935. It closes faster than light could cross it.

Chapter: 11

Enforcement court

In Swiss civil procedure, the court that enforces a judgment ordering a party to do something, refrain from something or tolerate something. It can threaten a criminal penalty or set a disciplinary fine of up to 1,000 francs for each day of non-compliance.

Part 3, chapter: 1

A measure of disorder. In a closed system it never decreases, which is the second law of thermodynamics.

The document in which a manufacturer declares, under its sole responsibility, that a medical device meets Regulation (EU) 2017/745. Annex IV of the regulation lists its contents, and a device of class III also needs certificates from a notified body. With it, the device carries the CE marking.

The court in Strasbourg that rules on complaints that a state has broken the European Convention on Human Rights, once the courts of that state have been tried.

A 2007 treaty between the European Union, Denmark, Iceland, Norway and Switzerland on jurisdiction in civil and commercial matters. Public deliberation

At the Swiss Federal Supreme Court, the procedure used when the judges of a panel disagree on a draft judgment. The parties do not speak.

Schabowski's note

On 9 November 1989 Günter Schabowski, a member of the East German Politburo, read out new rules for travel to the West at a press conference. Asked when they took effect, he answered "immediately", and the rules had been meant to start the next day. Crowds gathered at the crossings in Berlin, and the guards at Bornholmer Straße opened the gate.

Virtual machine

A whole computer simulated inside another, used to run software apart from the real system.

Chapters: 1, 2, 5

W

Wayland

The display protocol that replaced X11 on most Linux desktops. A program on another machine cannot draw on a Wayland screen by itself.

Western Ghats

The range of hills and mountains along the west coast of India. Near Pune they contain old hill forts that crowds visit during the monsoon.

Part 2, chapter: 2

White hole

A theoretical opposite of a black hole, which only lets things out. None has been observed.

Chapter: 11

Wormhole

A theoretical tunnel through space and time joining two distant places.

X

X11

Version 11 of the X Window System, the display system of Unix desktops since 1987. A program running on another machine can draw its windows on an X11 screen over the network.
