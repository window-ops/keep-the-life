What these values mean
  • Level 3, Confidential: staff with Level 3 clearance, such as Dr. Kellerman, may read the file.
  • Containment Class, Euclid: SCP-████ is a sapient human who has breached containment once, during Incident ████-1. He was Keter until regular interviews began.
  • Secondary Class, None: no esoteric class applies. Explained, the class under review, would replace the containment class.
  • Disruption Class, 2/Vlam: the implant affects only its bearer, and his knowledge of files up to Level 4 could reach a small group. Incident ████-1 stayed inside Site-██ and was contained.
  • Risk Class, 1/Notice: people near SCP-████ feel no effect and face no danger from him or from the implant.

Special Containment Procedures

SCP-████ is held at a temporary site pending the Overseer Council's review of containment, scheduled for 14 March 2030. No term for release applies, since amnestics have no lasting effect on SCP-████ and the implant cannot be removed under its supported procedures.

Amnestics are not to be administered to SCP-████. Repeated doses have produced tolerance and spill-over effects without clearing his memories (see Description).

SCP-████ is not to be exposed to cognitohazardous material until device testing is complete. His earlier result with SCP-2316 came from reading only, and testing has not confirmed that the device protects against images or sound. While the device remains Euclid, SCP-████ will not be transferred to other sites.

Magnetic resonance imaging requires approval from the medical officer. A magnetic sensor on the implant cuts its power inside the scanner, and the effect of a power cut on retrieval is undocumented.

Bench units of the implant are designated SCP-████-1. They are kept sealed in item storage, and removing one requires written approval from one staff member with Level 4 clearance. One SCP-████-1 unit was wiped, locked and powered off during Incident ████-2 and is now logged as an unused implant. A second unit is implanted in Researcher Pell (see Addendum ████-D).

Description

SCP-████ is a human male named Toma, a former programmer at AKV Solutions. The Foundation lists AKV Solutions as a dissolved Group of Interest. SCP-████ shows no anomalous properties of his own. His classification comes from the anti-amnestic implant he designed, which sits behind his right ear.

The implant copies memories to NAND storage outside the biological brain. When a memory is recalled, the function retrieve_recode skips the stored emotional (affect) channel and keeps only a pointer back to it. This produces two effects:

  1. After amnestic exposure, the subject keeps every memory the dose was meant to erase.
  2. Cognitohazards that work through emotional or subjective perception have little or no effect on stored material. SCP-████ was able to read SCP-2316 as a plain article.

Amnestic exposure leads the implant to mark memories with a delete flag. The flag is stored in tissue as a serotonin analog in which the terminal NH2 group is replaced by S-PO3H2 (C10H12NO4PS). The proposed designation for this compound is SCP-████-2. The flag clears whenever a marked memory is relearned with similarity above 0.72, and no function checks where the flag came from. Dr. I. Kellerman recorded this as a design omission in the refresh path. Left in the body, the compound cannot be cleared naturally and is expected to act like serotonin toxicity. Over months it is expected to cause 5-HT2B valve damage similar to that caused by fenfluramine.

The firmware is coreboot with the payload akv_recall 1.0-rc4, built as a separate image from akv_core. Both are parts of the akv package. The first string in the binary's read-only data is the warranty disclaimer of the GNU General Public License, which shows that AKV meant to release the implant as free software. The source repository and the circuit design files were destroyed.

The board has two areas. The test area has WRITE, READ and DEBUG pads behind a hardware switch. The usable area has the NAND chip and ten pins: VIN, GND, REF, RX0 to RX2, TX0, TX1, SNS_SP and SNS_T. Updates go through an inductive coil placed over the test pads. Document ████-2 shows how the parts work together. A cluster of unlabelled components in one corner holds the magnetic sensor. Very small text on the silkscreen beside it, estimated at under 0.3 mm in height and legible only under a bench microscope, reads "Magnetic Distortion System".

SCP-████ has extensive knowledge of the Foundation and of the anomalies it contains, up to Level 4. While at AKV Solutions, he followed the metadata of leaked Foundation documents to an unclassified copy of the Foundation's database that was reachable from outside. At its end was a sortable index of every anomaly at Level 4 and below. He was the only member of his team to look at the metadata. The gap that exposed the copy has since been closed. Since amnestics have no lasting effect on him, this knowledge cannot be removed.

SCP-████ states that his brain now depends on the implant, and he declines removal.

Addenda

Addendum ████-A: Reclassification

Date: ██/██/2029, before chapter 1

SCP-████ was downgraded from Keter to Euclid once regular interviews began. Under Keter, his meals came through a hatch in the cell door, at fixed times.

Addendum ████-B: Incident ████-1

Date: 7 August 2029, in chapter 3

During the June 2029 containment breach at Site-██, SCP-████ did the following:

  • He used an SQL injection on the canteen terminal to read his own file. The terminal checked input only on the client side.
  • He contacted SCP-079 through the site network and gave it a virtual machine. The original old_ai.safetensors.xz and SCP-079's UPLOAD.TXT were later recovered from it.
  • He built a local language model, called "Peachy", from SCP-079's port.

The canteen terminal was taken out of service after Dr. Kellerman's report.

Addendum ████-C: Device testing

Date: 20 December 2029, in chapter 11

Bench testing of SCP-████-1 units found ordinary NAND behavior:

  • Random writes stopped at the capacity printed on the die.
  • The unit keeps a bad block table and wore out on schedule.
  • A full wipe left it blank.
  • The delete flag behaved exactly as the disassembly predicts.

The Council signed the Level 4 authorization needed for cognitohazard testing. In the image test, Researcher Pell looked at the SCP-2316 photograph for ten minutes. He described a lake with bodies in it, recognized none of them, and did not move toward the water. The sound and text tests are pending.

Addendum ████-D: Incident ████-2

Date: 9 December 2029, after chapter 10

During the Site-██ loading yard incident, on the night of 1 December 2029, two SCP-████-1 units were taken from item storage to protect SCP-239 from a cognitohazard released by MTF Epsilon-11 (see Document ████-1).

The first unit ran on SCP-239 through electrodes. Using setup mode, SCP-████ returned the unit's stored memories to SCP-239, cleared the marker compound through her immune system, then wiped, locked and powered off the unit.

SCP-239 placed the second unit behind Researcher Pell's ear. Surgeons at a valley clinic later pushed it the rest of the way in, and it remains implanted.

Addendum ████-E: Ethics Committee decision on Project Cicero

Date: 5 November 2029, in chapter 6

Project Cicero turned the way SCP-████ talks to anomalies into interview guidelines. The Ethics Committee decided on the project at a remote hearing in conference room 3 of Site-██, chaired by Dr. T. Lindqvist. Points 6 and 7 of the decision concern SCP-████:

6. Toma: while the device remains Euclid, he will not be transferred to other sites. If the device is Explained, the Committee recommends that he shall not be assigned to anomalies he has declined, and that each assignment require his consent.

7. The Committee supports the request for a level four signature for the cognitohazard test on the device and forwards it with this decision.

Addendum ████-F: Attachments and affiliation

Date: 20 December 2029, in chapter 11

Staff have documented an attachment between SCP-████ and SCP-239 since their first audio session, during which SCP-239 offered to remove his implant and accepted his refusal. Site-██ later chose to keep regular contact between the two, and her revised procedure kept her shut down whenever SCP-████ was unavailable. Both are now held at the same temporary site.

The night log of the humanoid wing documents an attachment between SCP-████ and Dr. Iris Kellerman, the researcher assigned to his file. Her contract forbids friendships with anomalies. During his review of the file, Acting Site Director Dr. Pryce removed her from it pending a conflict-of-interest review based on the night log. The review was postponed, and Dr. Pryce was relieved pending review after Incident ████-2. The outcome of her review is not recorded.

SCP-████ is counted as part of the reformist faction, an informal group of Foundation staff with an inter-site working group. The working group took its motto, "Some hooligans are causing disorder", from his notes on Project Cicero. During Incident ████-2 he read the Habeas Corpus Act 1679 to the staff gathered in the loading yard. The temporary site where he is held is a camp in the Alps occupied by the faction.

Addendum ████-G: Report on the removal of the first unit

Date: 14 December 2029, after chapter 10

In the weeks after Incident ████-2, the containment engineer present at the removal of the first SCP-████-1 unit from SCP-239 wrote a report on it. The firmware version, akv_recall 1.0-rc4, and the serial number AKV-RC4-000193 indicate that the software is presumably the fourth release candidate of version 1.0. The report states that the removal was difficult and records the following faults:

  • Power off is not implemented in the default mode. A forced power off is available only in setup mode, through the DEBUG pad.
  • Power off uses g_mount_unmount, a GIO function deprecated since GLib 2.22, copied into the firmware.
  • Immune clearance of the marker compound stalled at 2 of 6 marked sites.
  • The synchronous unmount stalled in uninterruptible sleep, waiting for a lock the wipe had already taken. The firmware lists this as a known issue.
  • Ending the stalled process while it held the store lock crashed the unit. It restarted and saved an error state.
  • The unit then required safe mode and a check of its store with akvfsck before it reported an unused implant and could be shut down.

The report concludes that the device has bugs and is not perfect, and that its safeties exist because it can fail.

Addendum ████-H: Reclassification to Explained

Date: 2 December 2029, written during the move after chapter 10

If the device is reclassified as Explained, SCP-████ could be granted Level 4 clearance and become Class C personnel. In accordance with the Ethics Committee decision, he could instead become Class B personnel, since point 6 recommends that he not be assigned to anomalies he has declined. Some staff believe he would be valuable to the Foundation, and that the implant would be valuable to staff handling high-risk anomalies.

Should the device become Explained, it is not to be administered to D-Class personnel. It may be administered to E-Class personnel.

After Incident ████-2, the staff of Site-██ were moved to the temporary site, SCP-████ among them. The containment engineers wrote down their reasoning for these provisions while they were being moved, before they reached the temporary site:

  • Class C: amnestics have no lasting effect on SCP-████, so he can never be released, and Explained status would make him staff for life. He already knows files up to Level 4 and has worked in direct contact with SCP-079 and SCP-239. Class C would let him keep that contact, and one Level 4 clearance across files would let any team assign him.
  • Class B: SCP-████ has declined work with the most dangerous anomalies, and point 6 recommends that each assignment require his consent. His protection against cognitohazards is confirmed for reading only. Class B keeps him away from anomalies he has not agreed to and keeps his knowledge available to the teams.
  • Use of the device: the Foundation filed AKV Solutions as a Group of Interest for building a device it did not want built, since a chip that keeps what amnestics remove works against the veil outside the Foundation. The engineers judged that objection to be weaker, considering logistical needs. Long-serving staff on high-risk files already cannot leave with their memories, so the implant costs them little, and it protects them from cognitohazards that work through perception. During Incident ████-2, two units protected SCP-239 and Researcher Pell from such a cognitohazard. The faults they saw during the removal of the first unit (see Addendum ████-G) and the marker compound remain risks, so use is limited to cases where the protection outweighs them.
  • D-Class and E-Class: D-Class personnel receive amnestics at the end of their term, and the implant would defeat them. E-Class personnel have already been exposed to dangerous anomalies and are held under observation, so the implant adds protection without changing whether they can leave.

Attached documents

Document ████-1: Statement on the use of cognitohazards by MTF Epsilon-11

To: the Overseer Council; the Ethics Committee

From: the containment engineers of Site-██, at the temporary site

Date: 17 December 2029, after chapter 10

Concerning: Incident ████-2

  1. On the night of Incident ████-2, MTF Epsilon-11 was deployed to Site-██ by order of the Acting Site Director, Dr. Pryce, to restore normal operations. The personnel gathered in the canteen sector were unarmed Foundation staff. The Council has since handled the gathering as an internal grievance.
  2. Operatives released a cognitohazardous mass from a sealed drum at the door of the cell of SCP-239 on the lowest level, and further masses through the ventilation of the research wing. The mass appears to each viewer as the person they love most and draws the viewer toward it.
  3. SCP-239 was awake when the mass was released at her door. She held it off from her doorway with a force field and her bed rail until her energy was spent, and the site announced a containment breach. Personnel across the research wing were exposed without warning. The medical technician on her shift and one Epsilon-11 handler remain missing, and the site remains infested.
  4. The only protection available that night was the implant of SCP-████, a device under review whose protection against cognitohazards had been confirmed for reading only. Researcher Pell took two SCP-████-1 units from item storage. One ran on SCP-239 through electrodes. SCP-239 placed the other behind his ear, with no surgeon and no sterile conditions. Removing the first unit then required the steps recorded in Addendum ████-G.
  5. The engineers condemn the use of cognitohazards against Foundation personnel and against a contained humanoid. The deployment left the implant as the only protection and forced its use. That use is no evidence that the device is ready, and its cost to SCP-239 and to Researcher Pell is a consequence of the deployment.
  6. The engineers ask the Council to include the use of cognitohazards in its review of the orders given to MTF Epsilon-11.

Document ████-2: Functional diagram of the implant

From: the containment engineers of Site-██, at the temporary site

Date: 19 December 2029, after chapter 10

Based on: Dr. Kellerman's notebook pinout of the bench unit (chapter 7), extended with the functions found in the disassembly (chapter 1) and the behavior seen during the removal of the first unit (chapter 10)

  1. The pins keep the numbers of the notebook: 1 VIN, 2 GND, 3 REF, 4 RX0, 5 RX1, 6 RX2, 7 TX0, 8 TX1, 9 SNS_SP, 10 SNS_T. The brain reaches only these ten. WRITE, READ and DEBUG sit behind the hardware switch.
  2. The chip signals the tissue where and when to lay the marker at a write site, and SNS_SP reads it back. No brain makes the marker on its own.
  3. A recalled engram reaches the cortex without its affect channel. The store keeps that channel, and the engram keeps a pointer to it.
  4. Pins 5 and 9 run straight to an electrode in the tissue and to the sensor. A coil left over the usable area induces current in them and heats their tips, as a scanner field heats the leads of a deep brain stimulator.
  5. The function that writes a new engram to the store was not named in the disassembly. Outside setup mode, no function using TX1 was recovered.

Document ████-3: Sketch of the board from photographs

From: the containment engineers of Site-██, at the temporary site

Date: 18 December 2029, after chapter 10

Based on: photographs of unit S/N AKV-RC4-000193 under the bench microscope after its removal (chapter 10), laid out as in Dr. Kellerman's notebook sketch (chapter 7)

  1. The top side has the test area with its three pads and the switch, and the usable area with the NAND chip and ten pins. Each pin has a lead to an electrode or sensor in the tissue.
  2. Copper traces run on both sides. On the top they join the pads, the switch and the pins to the NAND chip. Vias carry the supply, the ground, the sensor line and pin 10 to the underside, which is drawn as seen from below and so mirrored.
  3. The cluster of eight components in the top right corner holds the magnetic sensor. No code reads it.
  4. The silkscreen beside the cluster reads "Magnetic Distortion System" and is legible only under the bench microscope.
  5. The underside contains the serial number and the unlock code in tiny print. The code is written once into the firmware and cannot be changed. It is redacted in this file.
  6. The photographs do not represent the dimensions of the board or exact components.

Document ████-4: Sketch of the bench kit

From: the containment engineers of Site-██, at the temporary site

Date: 18 December 2029, after chapter 10

Based on: the kit taken from item storage during Incident ████-2 (chapter 10)

  1. The reader is handheld and nearly all screen, a 4:3 display behind a thin dark rim. Three keys sit along the bottom edge of the case: CTRL and ALT on the left, the power key on the right. The screen shows the last state of unit S/N AKV-RC4-000193.
  2. A short cable joins the reader to a flat inductive coil. Laid on the skin over the board, the coil powers the chip through VIN. Laid on the DEBUG pad, it opens setup mode.
  3. The adhesive electrodes let a unit run outside the body, as on SCP-239 during Incident ████-2.
  4. Pressed alone in the default mode, the power button reports that power off is not implemented there. In setup mode, power off sits in the Power tab. Pressing CTRL, ALT and power together opens Program Manager, which lists the tasks running in the background, such as a stalled unmount.
