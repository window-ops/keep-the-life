1.1A parts list

Four months later, at the end of April, Toma stands on the shore of a small lake in the woods outside Reading, Ohio. The March review moved his file to Explained, and his first assignment was delivered to the camp with a consent form, which he signed. The containment zone is a ring of chain-link fence among the trees, patrolled by guards who have never been told what is inside it. Inside the fence a climbing rope runs from the harness at Toma's waist to a steel post behind him. Out on the lake, a little way from the shore, the bodies float together. Three researchers from the 2316 team sit on folding chairs facing him with their backs to the water, their own ropes tied to posts. Each has a healed pale line behind the ear, where their implants sit. A field recorder stands on a tripod between them with its red light on.

Kowalczyk

over the radio clipped to the shoulder strap of Toma's harness, from the gate Give the date and your designation, then tell them what's in front of you the way you'd read out a parts list. If a face starts to look like someone you know, you say "stop", you sit down in the grass, and we end the take, and I don't want an argument about it afterward.

Toma

tapping the radio to record his reply Alright.

Kowalczyk

over the radio, after the click of Toma's reply Copy. Go ahead.

Toma

staying still, looking at the lake SCP-2316 is a lake located just outside Reading, Ohio. It is known for its anomalous property of attracting anybody who knows about the lake into it, by inducing the belief that a relative may have died in the lake, even when it is extremely unlikely for the said relative to have died in the lake since they were outside the village or the country. For comparison, I need a description of Reading, Pennsylvania.

Yoder

the man on the left chair, gray in his beard, glancing at the recorder and then back up at Toma The file says people you knew as a kid, mostly. I grew up thirty minutes outside Reading, Pennsylvania. It's in Berks County, about ninety-five thousand people along the Schuylkill River, an hour and a bit northwest of Philadelphia. Brick row houses, a Japanese pagoda up on Mount Penn that they light at night, the railroad off the Monopoly board, and pretzel bakeries. From here it's about eight hundred kilometers east.

Toma

Good. Given that Reading, Ohio is so far apart from Reading, Pennsylvania, it is unlikely for someone to even have heard of this village. Anyways, in the lake lie the bodies of people who have thrown themselves into the lake. The depth is approximately forty-five meters, although the exact number is unknown due to the fact that bodies form their own layer which cannot be removed. They are believed to form an expanding hive consciousness. We don't know what is the purpose of this hive consciousness, or even if it exists.

Hollis

the woman on the right chair, writing on a clipboard on her knee without looking up Forty-five is the body count, the low estimate, and some go as high as two hundred. The probes measured past thirty meters near the middle and then were lost. The count goes up every time someone touches the water, and a hand is enough. looking up at him Whether they jumped is still open. The DNA came back inconclusive.

Toma

The bodies that I see are not identifiable. They are pale and scaly. They are never let to decompose naturally. They tend to float near the shore. I don't see any further bodies out.

Kowalczyk

over the radio Pick the closest one and stay on it. How far out is it, and what does the face look like? Eyes open or closed. And "scaly" how, like a fish, or like skin that's been in the water too long?

Toma

moving his eyes to the closest body The closest body I can see is ten meters out, eyes half-open, and like skin that has been in the water for too long.

Kowalczyk

over the radio Move off the eyes and give me the rest of it, hair, clothes, anything on the hands. Listeners, if any of what he's saying starts to sound like someone you know, you lift a hand and keep it up.

Toma

Hollis

Hollis

If you cry about it, it's drama, and if you take her to a doctor, the whole building knows by evening.

Hollis

Toma

Hollis

Do not take within six hours of driving. lowering her hand There was a red line on the side of the strip, which means prescription only, and the chemist sold it to her across the counter like chewing gum.

Toma

Hollis

starting to cry again I'd ban all of them, the small doses too. Trauma is part of the person who lived it, and you take that away and call it treatment. pressing the back of her wrist to her mouth for a moment And I work for the people who make them.

Toma

The Amnestics Department.

Hollis

nodding, wiping her face I was in it, at your old site. I checked dosing tables for field teams, how many milligrams for a witness, how many for a whole street. looking down at her hands The week after the yard I asked for a transfer, and the first thing they offered me was this chip and this lake.

1.5Black and white

Hollis

Hollis

Toma

Hollis

taking a long breath and looking past him at the fence A lot of things at once. I'm angry at the chemist, and at myself. I'm glad the chip worked, and I'm scared of how glad I am. wiping her cheeks with both hands The crying sits on top of all of it, so people see a girl crying and never ask.

Hollis

Hollis

Hollis

Toma

Right.

Hollis

I'd like to work with you, if they let me. with a short laugh, wiping the last tear The people who argue with you the most are usually the ones who want to help build the thing.

heading toward the truck

2.1The ordinary part

At night the team's plane climbs out over Ohio and turns east for Geneva. The plane is a small jet with eight leather seats and a folding table between each pair. Two hours after takeoff the cabin lights are dimmed. Yoder sleeps in the front row with his jacket over his face, Reyes watches the dark through his window with his headphones on, and Kowalczyk sits alone at the back with the sealed bag on the table in front of him and a laptop open beside it. Hollis has pulled a black sweater over her field shirt and let her curls down, and she sits next to Toma with her knees drawn up on the seat and a paper cup of tea going cold in her hands.

Hollis

turning her head on the headrest to look at him Can I ask you something that isn't about the lake, like, what do you miss most?

Toma

Being outside of all of this.

Hollis

nodding slowly, looking at the tea instead of him Me too. after a moment What did outside look like for you? I mean the ordinary part.

Toma

Working at my website, playing games. I've liked No Man's Sky.

Hollis

frowning a little, the cup still in both hands I don't know that one. turning in the seat to face him What is it, and what was the website?

Toma

No Man's Sky is a game where you explore space. There are three main intergalactic civilizations and then there is Atlas, who is believed to be a god by the Korvax and believed to not be a god by the Vy'keen, who are in dispute with the Gek. Atlas is revealed to be creator of the universe, which is in fact a simulation. And my website was well, a personal website where I published programs and stuff.

Hollis

smiling a little at the cup So the whole game is a simulation, and the people inside it argue about whether its maker is a god. after a moment, her smile fading I never had anything like that, a website or a game I loved. As a teenager I was like everyone else in my class.

Toma

And how did you get here?

Hollis

setting the cold tea down on the folding table Pharmacy, first. I did my degree in Pune, then a master's in pharmacology in Manchester, because it was the furthest away my parents would pay for. turning the cup a little on the table In the last term a company came to recruit, with a warehouse and a pharmaceutical name on the gate, and they said they needed people for dosing work. I signed the contract, and the first week underground they told me what the doses were for.

Toma

And why didn't you leave early, when you could?

Hollis

letting out a short breath that is almost a laugh Leaving early meant they dose you on the way out. I'd have gone home missing a whole year and a half, like my friend, and I'd have taken the strip myself to get there. looking at the dark window past him And my parents had told the whole building their daughter worked for a big company abroad. So I stayed and told myself I'd make the doses smaller from inside.

Toma

You really care what the whole building thinks about you?

Hollis

turning back to him My parents do, and I care about my parents. more softly, pulling the sweater sleeves down over her hands When the building talks, my mother hears it at the vegetable stall every morning. I'd rather lie to the building than burden her with that.

2.5No protocol

Toma

My father had a SRL, which made him some money out of experimental psychological therapy. He was an exceptional therapist. For every patient he had personalized therapy.

Hollis

tilting her head What's an SRL?

Toma

Societate cu Răspundere Limitată, Limited Liability Company. It's a type of company which, in contrast to the sole proprietorship, allows for the easier division between the business and the person. This means that if you go in debt, creditors cannot sell what you personally own. Additionally, you can only lose your share in the company.

Hollis

Like a private limited at home, Pvt Ltd on the signboard. My father's auto was in his own name, so when he missed payments it was the auto they came for. turning on the seat toward him Your father's therapy, though. What made it experimental?

Toma

The fact that he never followed a specific standardized therapy.

Hollis

No protocol at all? In pharmacology that would give me nightmares. after a moment, curious How did he know it was working, then, without a standard to measure against?

Toma

Well, he explained to me that the purpose of therapy is to place the stone, the problem, in front. The patient describes the problem as they feel right now. Then, the therapist has to find the specific type of error in their argument, and then you try to break down the error in the argument, and the further you delve into the error, the more likely you are going to find the problem. And then once you found the problem, you use some psycho-education. Of course that's one model. He had many models for many types of patients, and since some patients did try standard treatments that didn't work, he skips those and tries alternative methods. He is flexible about which specific method to use.

Hollis

listening with her chin on her knees, then lifting it That sounds like the cognitive therapy they taught us a little of in Manchester, finding the mistake in how someone thinks. Only he chose the method to fit the person.

Toma

Yes, and more importantly, you never fully rely on the person to reframe, and you never proceed like therapy bots, programs designed to catch every single non-significant omission. You focus on things that are not temporary mistakes.

Hollis

thinking about it And how do you tell which mistakes are temporary?

Toma

Hollis

Toma

Of course.
